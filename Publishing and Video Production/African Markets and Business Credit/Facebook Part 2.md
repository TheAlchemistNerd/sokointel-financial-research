# A loan that follows the way the business earns

Suppose a business has a good order and a customer who pays after delivery. The owner needs money now for materials and production. A loan can help complete the order, but its usefulness depends on more than how quickly the application is approved. When does the first repayment fall due? How much cash actually reaches the business after fees? What amount leaves the account at each repayment date? Those questions connect the loan to the activity it is supposed to finance. We can place the expected customer receipts and the proposed repayments on the same calendar, then see where the business needs additional cash. The comparison becomes easier to discuss because it follows the way the business actually earns.

The second article in our African Markets and Business Credit series explores this connection through banks, loan pricing and fintech. A benchmark rate provides one reference point. The premium, fees and repayment structure determine the rest of the borrowing experience. Payment services can help money move and improve transaction records. Working-capital credit can help bridge a collection gap. Longer-term finance can support equipment whose earnings arrive over several years. Each service has a useful role when its terms fit the activity being financed. The article compares worked examples and research findings, then asks what owners and lenders can measure to understand whether the financing supports durable business growth.

You can begin with two offers and a single page. Record the amount received, every fee, the repayment amounts and their dates. Add the customer receipts the loan is expected to help generate. Compare the offers against the same order and the same starting cash balance. If the collection date moves, update the calendar and see what changes. This turns the conversation into a practical discussion of timing, cost and the business’s ability to complete the work. The full article, **Making Business Credit Work in Kenya**, includes the calculations and references. When you compare business finance, which feature has made the greatest difference for you: the total cost, the repayment timing, or continued access when the next order arrives?
