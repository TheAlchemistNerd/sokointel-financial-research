# Appendix 2 — Statement models, financial quality, and valuation

The manufacturer, bank, and insurance scenarios are fictional teaching cases. Their figures are not valuations of the named NSE issuers in the income screen. All currency amounts below are KES million unless identified as per-share values. Excel formulas and mathematical equivalents appear in the generated dictionary at the end.

## A2.1 The integrated manufacturer

Opening balances at31December2026 are revenue10,000; receivables1,200; inventory1,000; payables900; net plant6,000; cash1,200; debt2,500; and equity6,000. Assets = 1,200 + 1,000 + 6,000 + 1,200 = 9,400. Liabilities plus equity = 900 + 2,500 + 6,000 = 9,400. Shares outstanding are1,000million.

The forecast assumptions have a separate input for every year2027–2031. Annual growth, EBITDA margin, depreciation, capex, tax, collection days, inventory days, supplier days, cost of sales, interest, principal repayment, and payout all feed the statements. Interest uses opening debt, payments occur at year-end, and the case has no new borrowing, share issuance, acquisitions, leases, tax-loss carryforwards, or other comprehensive income. Those features can be added as separate schedules when modelling an actual company.

For2027:

> Revenue = 10,000 × 1.06 = **10,600**
> COGS = 10,600 × 0.60 = **6,360**
> EBITDA = 10,600 × 0.22 = **2,332**
> Depreciation = 6,000 × 0.10 = **600**
> EBIT = 2,332 − 600 = **1,732**
> Interest = 2,500 × 0.12 = **300**
> Profit before tax = 1,732 − 300 = **1,432**
> Tax = 1,432 × 0.30 = **429.60**
> Net profit = 1,432 − 429.60 = **1,002.40**

EBITDA is earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, and amortisation; EBIT is operating profit after depreciation in this case. The model forecasts EBITDA from a margin; COGS is used to determine the working-capital balances. Remaining operating costs are implicit in the EBITDA margin rather than separately forecast.

## A2.2 Working capital and cash conversion

Article equation (2.1):

> NWC = trade receivables + inventory − trade payables
> AR = revenue × collection days /365
> Inventory = COGS × inventory days /365
> AP = COGS × supplier days /365
> ΔNWC = closing NWC − opening NWC

The forecast uses closing operating balances, whereas the historical sector screen uses averages to calculate observed days. COGS is explicitly used as a purchases proxy for supplier days. Exclude cash, interest-bearing debt, and nontrade balances from operating NWC unless their economic role justifies a separately documented adjustment.

> AR2027 = 10,600 ×44/365 = **1,277.80822**
> Inventory2027 = 6,360 ×60/365 = **1,045.47945**
> AP2027 = 6,360 ×50/365 = **871.23288**
> NWC2027 = 1,277.80822 +1,045.47945 −871.23288 = **1,452.05479**
> NWC2026 = 1,200 +1,000 −900 = **1,300**
> ΔNWC = **152.05479**

Historical cash-conversion-cycle formulas are DIO = average inventory/COGS ×365; DSO = average trade receivables/revenue ×365; DPO = average trade payables/COGS ×365; and CCC = DIO +DSO −DPO. For the efficient-cycle example, DIO = 500/6,000×365 =30.4167days; DSO =300/10,000×365 =10.95days; DPO =1,200/6,000×365 =73days. Thus CCC = **−31.6333days**. Investigate whether the supplier terms, collections, and stock levels can persist.

## A2.3 Cash, plant, debt, and equity roll-forwards

> CFO = net profit +depreciation −ΔNWC
> CFO2027 = 1,002.40 +600 −152.05479 = **1,450.34521**
> Capex2027 = 10,600×0.09 = **954**
> Cash after capex = 1,450.34521 −954 = **496.34521**
> Dividend = 1,002.40×0.40 = **400.96**
> Closing cash = 1,200 +1,450.34521 −954 −250 −400.96 = **1,045.38521**
> Closing plant = 6,000 +954 −600 = **6,354**
> Closing debt = 2,500 −250 = **2,250**
> Closing equity = 6,000 +1,002.40 −400.96 = **6,601.44**

Article equation (2.2) is the cash-after-capex calculation. In this example CFO includes interest through net profit. It is distinct from unlevered free cash flow used in enterprise valuation.

The2027 balance sheet reconciles: assets =1,277.80822 +1,045.47945 +6,354 +1,045.38521 =**9,722.67288**. Liabilities plus equity =871.23288 +2,250 +6,601.44 =**9,722.67288**. The model repeats the roll-forward through2031, with a residual tolerance for floating-point arithmetic. A negative calculated cash balance represents an unfunded plan requiring an explicit financing or operating response.

## A2.4 Unlevered cash flow and DCF

| Symbol | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| FCFF | Free cash flow to the firm, before financing payments |
| NOPAT | Operating profit after the assumed operating tax |
| w | Weighted average cost of capital, nominalKES |
| g | Stable terminal growth, nominalKES |
| ROIC | Return on newly invested operating capital in the terminal state |
| EV | Enterprise value |
| NCI | Minority interests or other ownership claims deducted in the equity bridge |

> NOPAT = EBIT(1 −tax rate)
> FCFF = NOPAT +depreciation −capex −ΔNWC
> FCFF2027 = 1,732×0.70 +600 −954 −152.05479 = **706.34521**
> PV_forecast = Σ[t=1…5] FCFFₜ/(1+w)^t
> Terminal reinvestment fraction = g/ROIC
> FCFF₆ = NOPAT₅(1+g)(1−g/ROIC)
> TV₅ = FCFF₆/(w−g)
> EV = PV_forecast +TV₅/(1+w)^5
> Equity value = EV +excess cash +nonoperating investments −debt −NCI
> Value per share = equity value /shares

At w=14%, g=4%, ROIC=16%, terminal reinvestment is25%. The workbook calculates PV_forecast **3,358.04817**, terminal NOPAT **1,626.33215**, terminal FCFF **1,219.74911**, terminal EV **12,197.49109**, and terminal PV **6,334.99466**. EV is **9,693.04283**. Adding1,200cash and deducting2,500debt gives equity value **8,393.04283**, or **KES 8.39304/share**.

Article equation (2.4): MOS =1−price/value. At illustrative price6, MOS =1−6/8.39304 =**28.5122%**. Terminal PV represents **65.3561%** of EV. The sensitivity grid recomputes both forecast discounting and terminal reinvestment for each pair of w and g. Require w>g, ROIC>0, positive shares, and economically supportable growth; a terminal retention fraction above100% needs a different steady-state structure.

## A2.5 Bank diagnostics and distribution capacity

> NPL ratio = nonperforming loans /gross loans
> Coverage = impairment allowance /NPL stock
> Cost of risk = annual impairment charge /average gross loans
> NIM = net interest income /average earning assets
> CIR = operating expense /operating income
> Capital ratio = eligible regulatory capital /risk-weighted assets
> Headroom = actual capital ratio −applicable requirement

For the fictional diagnostic base: NPL ratio =8,000/80,000 =10%; coverage =5,000/8,000 =62.5%; cost of risk =1,200/77,000 =1.5584%; NIM =6,000/95,000 =6.3158%; CIR =3,500/10,000 =35%. Core capital ratio =11,000/80,000 =13.75%, giving3.25percentage points over a10.5% input. Total capital ratio =15,000/80,000 =18.75%, giving4.25points over14.5%. Requirements are entity-specific inputs; the exercise is not a regulatory determination for a named bank.

For the capacity model:

> Required retained capital = max[0,RWA₀(1+growth_RWA)×required ratio −eligible capital₀]
> Dividend capacity = max[0,scenario profit −required retained capital]

The proxy assumes opening equity equals eligible capital and omits regulatory deductions, additional buffers, supervisory restrictions, and subsidiary-transfer constraints. Add those explicitly for an actual bank.

The separate steady-state valuation uses P/B =(ROE−g)/(kₑ−g), derived from dividend retention g/ROE under clean-surplus assumptions. At ROE18%, g5%, kₑ16%, P/B =13/11 =**1.1818times**. Opening equity10,000 gives value11,818.18. The long-run ROE assumption is separate from the one-year profit stress, which may differ during the transition. A multi-stage residual-income model values B₀ +Σ[(ROEₜ−kₑ)Bₜ₋₁/(1+kₑ)^t] plus any terminal residual-income value.

## A2.6 Insurance bridge and asset value

Article equation (2.3), for the defined non-life example:

> Combined ratio = claims/earned premium +expenses/earned premium
> Underwriting result = earned premium −claims −expenses
> PBT = underwriting +investment +other −finance cost
> PAT = PBT −max(PBT,0)×tax rate
> ROE = PAT /[(opening equity+closing equity)/2]

At premiums4,000, claims2,600 and expenses1,080, the combined ratio is92% and underwriting profit320. Add880investment result and deduct100finance cost: PBT1,100. Tax330 leaves PAT770; average equity10,250 gives **7.5122% ROE**. This is a complete simplified profit bridge; investment income alone does not determine underwriting quality. Map IFRS17 service results and reinsurance to the actual issuer's definitions before applying it.

Adjusted NAV can be expressed as reported attributable equity plus documented asset-value adjustments minus additional liability or tax adjustments. Price/adjusted book then compares market equity value with that estimate. Avoid adding the value of a subsidiary twice when it is already consolidated. For life insurers, analyse the contractual service margin, reserve assumptions, cash emergence, and capital requirements separately from a short-tail combined ratio.

## A2.7 Historical quality signals

The workbook's five-year history is fictional and separate from the forecast. Its nine-signal screen is inspired by Piotroski but uses closing-asset denominators and the stated simplified definitions. It tests positive profitability, positive cash generation, improving profitability, cash exceeding accrual profit, lower leverage, improved liquidity, no share issuance, improved gross margin, and improved asset turnover. Each condition contributes0or1. This educational variant should not be presented as an exact replication of the original empirical strategy.
