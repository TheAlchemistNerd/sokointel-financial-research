# Appendix 4 — Compounding, Monte Carlo, and performance

Two experiments are supplied: a live 500-path aggregate-portfolio spreadsheet and a reproducible 10,000-path asset-level Python experiment. Their distributions and crisis definitions differ and are labelled separately. The extended results are snapshots; the workbook formulas update immediately only within their stated dependency chain.

## A4.1 Annual wealth and constant-return compounding

Article equation (4.1), with return earned before year-end external flows:

> Availableₜ =Wₜ₋₁(1+rₜ)+Cₜ
> Unfunded withdrawalₜ =max(0,Xₜ−Availableₜ)
> Wₜ =max(0,Availableₜ−Xₜ)

W is invested wealth, r the net annual return, C contribution, and X requested withdrawal. A negative balance is not treated as free borrowing. The deterministic sheet records the shortfall separately.

For constant r, C, zero withdrawals, and n years:

> Wₙ =W₀(1+r)^n +C[(1+r)^n−1]/r

The second term is the future value of an ordinary annuity, with contributions at each period-end. At r=0, the limit is W₀+nC. Beginning-of-period contributions multiply the annuity term by(1+r). This expands article equation(4.2).

Balanced gross expected return=.10455. Gross equity dividend yield under the stated price and ordinary-DPS inputs is 0.0588840048840 (5.88840048840%). Multiplying by the 5% dividend withholding rate gives a tax drag of 0.0029442002442 (0.29442002442 percentage points). The annual implementation drag is.003. The resulting net return is **.0986057997558**. Thus:

> W₂₀ =1,000,000(1.0986057997558)^20
> +120,000[(1.0986057997558)^20−1]/.0986057997558
> = **KES 13,324,123.24**

The dividend yield is already represented in total return. Only the tax drag is deducted; the model does not add the gross dividend a second time. The cost input is a constant proportional allowance, not an exact turnover-cost calculation for each annual rebalance.

## A4.2 Inflation and the real target

Article equation(4.3):

> Price index Iₙ =∏[t=1…n](1+πₜ)
> Real wealth =Wₙ/Iₙ
> Nominal target at n =real target×Iₙ

For constant5% inflation over20years, I₂₀=1.05^20=**2.6532977051**. Deterministic real wealth=13,324,123.24/2.6532977051=**KES 5,021,721.92**. A real KES 3m goal requires nominal wealth **KES 7,959,893.12** at year20. Contributions remain nominal unless the annual input schedule increases them.

## A4.4 Moment-matched lognormal returns

Let μ be an assumed arithmetic mean return greater than−1 and σ its standard deviation. For positive gross return G=1+R, choose:

> s²=ln[1+σ²/(1+μ)²]
> a=ln(1+μ)−s²/2
> G=exp(a+sZ), Z∼N(0,1)

Then E[G]=1+μ and SD(G)=σ. These moments apply before crisis adjustments, cost drag, and dividend tax. The workbook uses fixed normal draws and fixed uniform draws generated from NumPy seed 20260908, allowing the same random values to be reused after changing inputs. The500paths each contain20annual steps.

For the aggregate crisis experiment:

> Jₜ=1{Uₜ<p}; p=.08
> Gross factor after crisis=Gₜ(1−.25Jₜ)
> Net factor=max(0,gross factor−implementation drag−dividend tax drag)

The independent crisis adjustment reduces the unconditional expected gross factor to(1+μ)(1−p×.25). It is not mean-compensated. The year-end cash-flow recurrence then applies. The aggregate portfolio crisis affects the whole portfolio in this worksheet; the asset-level engine uses the separate losses below.

## A4.5 Asset-level dependence and crisis regime

The extended engine uses10,000paths,20years, and seed 20260909. It generates nine independent standard-normal draws per path-year: one common factor and eight residual factors. For asset i:

> Zᵢ=lᵢZ_common+√(1−lᵢ²)εᵢ
> Gᵢ=exp(aᵢ+sᵢZᵢ)

The latent-normal correlation is lᵢlⱼ. After the exponential transformation, the exact simple-return covariance is:

> Cov(Gᵢ,Gⱼ)=(1+μᵢ)(1+μⱼ)[exp(sᵢsⱼρᵢⱼ)−1]

It therefore differs slightly from directly applying the workbook03 arithmetic-return covariance matrix. The engine states this distinction rather than presenting the two models as an exact calibration match.

The shared crisis has annual probability8%, independent across years. Additional proportional gross-factor losses are35%,40%,30%,25%,20%,30%,30%,0% for EQTY,KCB,COOP,SCOM,BAT,JUB,KEGN,bills respectively. Each year the portfolio is rebalanced to its policy weights, then its weighted gross factor is reduced by.3% implementation drag and the assumed recurring-dividend withholding drag. End-year contributions and withdrawals follow. The bill return assumption already includes bill tax.

The model omits persistent regimes, conditional volatility, company default, market closure, state-dependent dividend yield, detailed trading lots, and exact turnover costs. These are explicit extensions for company-specific work. Joint stress scenarios complement the simulation where an event can be described more credibly than its probability estimated.

## A4.6 Distribution summaries and sampling precision

> Goal success p̂ =number of paths with W₂₀≥real goal×I₂₀ divided by N
> Sampling SE =√[p̂(1−p̂)/N]
> Approximate95% sampling interval =p̂±1.96SE

For Balanced/crisis: p̂=.6064 and N=10,000. SE=√(.6064×.3936/10,000)=**.00488548**, or**0.48855percentage points**. The approximate interval is**59.6824%–61.5976%**. It describes finite-sample uncertainty under the fixed model. It does not include uncertainty in returns, inflation, crisis probabilities, or the model specification.

## A4.7 Drawdown, tail loss, and spending gaps

The engine creates a unitised investment index Uₜ=Uₜ₋₁×net factorₜ, starting at1. Contributions and withdrawals are excluded from that index. Peakₜ=max(U₀,…,Uₜ); drawdownₜ=1−Uₜ/Peakₜ; maximum drawdown=maxₜdrawdownₜ. Results use annual checkpoints and therefore do not measure intrayear extremes.

Terminal unit loss is L=1−U₂₀. Empirical95% expected shortfall is the average of the largest500losses across10,000paths. Negative expected shortfall under this definition means the selected tail has an average terminal gain relative to initial unit value. A goal-shortfall loss max(0,goal−real wealth) is a different metric and can be substituted for a goal-focused analysis. VaR is the loss threshold; expected shortfall measures average severity in the selected tail.

Withdrawal-gap probability is the fraction of paths with at least one requested withdrawal above available funds. In the Balanced crisis case with no contributions and annualKES100,000withdrawals, this is**59.04%**. The zero-wealth floor prevents an unfunded payment from becoming an implicit loan. Future contributions could rebuild wealth after exhaustion in a different cash-flow schedule.

## A4.8 Time-weighted and money-weighted performance

The Performance worksheet has three annual subperiods with valuations immediately before external flows. Returns are10%,−5%,10%. Their linked cumulative return is:

> TWR=(1.10×.95×1.10)−1=**14.95%**
> Annualised TWR=(1.1495)^(1/3)−1

The investor contributesKES200,000after year1, withdrawsKES100,000after year2, and ends year3 withKES1,248,500. Investment gain=1,248,500−1,000,000−200,000+100,000=**KES 148,500**. Equal-annual-interval IRR solves:

> 0=−1,000,000−200,000/(1+r)+100,000/(1+r)²+1,248,500/(1+r)³

For actual dates dᵢ, XIRR solves ΣCFᵢ/(1+r)^[(dᵢ−d₀)/365]=0. A sign change is necessary for an ordinary investment IRR, and multiple sign changes can create multiple roots. TWR requires valuations around external flows; money-weighted return requires dated flows and a terminal value. Use a consistent treatment of dividends retained inside the account and costs deducted from the account.

## A4.9 Reproducibility and extensions

`Research/reproduce_models.py` rebuilds the model specification, formula audits, sampled-search results, and extended simulation results using NumPy. It does not execute trades or refresh market quotes. Preserve the input version, seed, cash-flow convention, and result file together. The existing XLSX extended-results tab remains a saved snapshot until rebuilt; the live 500-path spreadsheet calculation is independent.

A historical extension would add a point-in-time total-return dataset and a rolling estimation window, then execute rebalances using only information available at each date. A block bootstrap can resample contiguous historical periods to retain some temporal dependence. A regime model can make crisis persistence explicit. Parameter uncertainty can be tested by drawing plausible means, volatilities, or crisis probabilities before drawing return paths. These extensions answer different questions from simply increasing the number of paths in an unchanged model.
