## Purpose and teaching approach

This nine-episode course follows the written series in order. It begins with how a market exposure is defined and ends with the household processes that keep a portfolio useful. Corporate finance, fund structure, issuer economics and portfolio risk remain connected to household questions: funding, currency, tax, liquidity, contribution, withdrawal and disciplined review.

Each episode should be 10 to 18 minutes. Present a clear practical question, define the relevant terms, work through one table or formula, show the source date for factual inputs, and finish with a decision record or review rule. The workbook is the place for cell-level Excel instruction. The video should explain the model and demonstrate an input-to-output workflow.

## Course trailer

Create a 60 to 90 second trailer that explains the sequence: market and benchmark, wrapper and cost, ownership and tax, architecture, broad core, satellites, options, governance and long-term maintenance. Make clear that the course teaches a decision process rather than a model portfolio for every viewer.

## Required visual assets

- The directional strategy-layer graphic from Part 4.
- A simple return and drawdown chart with clearly labelled illustrative inputs.
- A fund-wrapper map that separates legal domicile, exchange listing, trading currency and underlying exposure.
- A look-through overlap table for Part 5.
- Semiconductor concentration and stress-test tables for Part 6.
- Put and covered-call payoff diagrams for Part 7.
- A four-sleeve allocation, risk-contribution and rebalancing dashboard for Part 8.
- A cash-flow timeline for contributions, reserves and withdrawals in Part 9.

Use a source marker on every dated fund statistic, company fact, fee or tax rule. Use no proprietary platform screenshots unless the terms allow it. Redraw tables and diagrams in the Sokointel visual style where possible.

## Recording and review checklist

1. Identify illustrations as illustrations and explain their assumptions.
2. Explain formulas in spoken language before showing workbook calculations.
3. Keep corporate-finance analysis connected to the investor consequence, such as concentration, cash-flow resilience, tax, liquidity or valuation risk.
4. Do not display live account balances, personal identifiers, credentials or private client records.
5. Add captions, chapters, transcript, article link, workbook link where applicable and source note.
6. Check arithmetic, source dates, claims, audio, captions and visual accessibility before release.

## Publishing the episode in the blog

1. Upload the approved video to the official YouTube channel.
2. In Django Admin, open **Research articles** and select the associated USMPC article.
3. Keep **Video planned** selected while production is under way. When the video is public, paste the YouTube watch, share, shorts or embed URL into **Video URL** and save.
4. The article will replace its preparation notice with a responsive, privacy-enhanced YouTube embed. The Watch page will mark the episode as available.
5. Open the public article at desktop and mobile widths. Confirm the title, video, chapter sequence, links and description agree with the published episode.

The blog accepts public YouTube links only. A malformed or non-YouTube link does not render an iframe, protecting readers from an unreviewed embedded destination.
