# Failure Retrospectives and Warning Systems — Video Tutorial Guide

Updated 24 September 2026. This guide plans three stand-alone episodes based on Parts 11–13. It is a production document, not a recorded script, finished slide deck or published video.

## Audience, voice and format

The audience is a founder, director, lender, investor, adviser or researcher who needs to distinguish an early warning from a later outcome. Speak as a careful investigator reconstructing what a specific decision-maker could know at a specific time. Use the exact legal entity, date, source type and evidence status. A court record may establish a procedural order or repeat a party’s claim; those are different kinds of statements.

Plan 16–20 minutes per episode. Treat the real-company timelines and amounts as dated evidence. Treat the workbook’s cash and retail schedules as fictional teaching examples, not financial results for Nakumatt, Tuskys, Twiga or Copia. Do not infer investor loss from a funding headline or failure cause from a later legal event.

## Course trailer

A 60–90 second trailer can ask: what would an owner have seen early enough to act? Preview the decision-date timeline, a retail cash-cycle review, separate legal entities and a warning-to-action register. Explain that a useful warning buys time for a defined response; it does not prove fraud, insolvency or the cause of a collapse.

### OE3-11 — The retrospective method

**Viewer question:** How can a board or lender test an early warning without rewriting history?  
**Runtime:** 16–18 minutes.

| Time | Screen and teaching beat | Evidence / workbook route |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–1:30 | Begin at a real decision date. Ask what the owner, lender or supplier knew then—not what a later report revealed. | Part 11 opening; Appendix 01 protocol. |
| 1:30–5:00 | Build an event line with separate event date, date known, evidence type and later source. Move one record to its public publication or filing date and show how the information set changes. | Timeline!C5:I13; preserve the separate columns and source IDs. |
| 5:00–8:00 | Classify one amount as a party claim, administrator-reported receipt, court finding, secondary report or missing figure. Ask what document would upgrade its status. | Evidence Ledger!C5:J14. Do not convert a claim into an admitted debt or cash balance. |
| 8:00–12:00 | Connect a signal to a specific owner and action: minimum cash floor, disputed major customer, aged stock or funding milestone. Add a response time and a record that would confirm or clear the signal. | Warnings and Actions!C5:I14. Thresholds are company-specific, not universal failure scores. |
| 12:00–15:00 | Test false alarms and missed cases. Explain what other healthy or recoverable business condition could produce the same signal, and what evidence would change the decision. | Part 11 “Test against false alarms and missed cases”; Appendix 01. |
| 15:00–18:00 | Close with a repeatable retrospective file: entity, date, source, measure, information available then, action, owner and outcome. | Evidence Vectors and the article’s case-file template. |

**Screen discipline:** Show a later legal outcome only after the earlier information set has been reconstructed. The warning should trigger a conversation or action, not a verdict about cause.

## Production and evidence rules

- On-screen record cards should carry a claim/evidence label: filed fact, court finding, party claim, administrator-reported figure, secondary report, analyst sum, fictional scenario or evidence gap.
- Never merge related entities because they share a brand. Preserve the entity names used in the legal record and label the scope of each figure.
- Distinguish event date, date known, source publication/filing date and retrieval/as-of date. Later evidence may explain an earlier event; it must not be shown as if known to the earlier decision-maker.
- Keep announcements, signed rounds, closed deals, funds drawn and cash received as different financing states. The USD185.4m aggregate is not company cash or investor loss.
- Use primary court and Gazette documents when available. Where this guide cites a secondary reproduction or a document locator remains incomplete, show that evidence gap and name the record to obtain.
- Cite peer-reviewed work through the article’s references when explaining retrospective bias, distress prediction or cash-cycle mechanisms. Do not turn an empirical association into a universal alarm threshold.
- Cite the matching appendix and workbook locator in speaker notes. Keep fictional workbook cells visibly labelled and keep real legal evidence on a separate visual lane.
