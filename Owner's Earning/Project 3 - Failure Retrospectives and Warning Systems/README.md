## Part-by-part research and workbook breakdown

### Part 11 — The retrospective method

**Decision:** Was a warning visible at the time, or only after the outcome was known? **Research:** period owner earnings and burn; DSO, DIO, DPO and supplier arrears; unrestricted funding runway and debt claims; governance decisions; customer and supplier behaviour; alternative explanations. **Article path:** define an evidence hierarchy and contemporaneous-information cutoffs before any case. Build a common cash bridge, CCC table, funding register, decision timeline and counterparty map. Mark fact, management claim, secondary report, analyst estimate, hypothesis and missing data separately. “Insufficient evidence” is a legitimate conclusion. **Workbook modules:** `Case Register`, `Timeline`, `Evidence Ledger`, `Funding`, `Cash and CCC`, `Warnings and Actions`, and `Sources and Checks`. The cash schedule is a fictional stress example, not reconstructed company history. **Evidence gate:** use court, administrator, audited, regulator, contract and company records ahead of narrative reports.

### Part 12 — Nakumatt and Tuskys retail retrospectives

**Decision:** Did stock, supplier financing, expansion and governance make the retail cash cycle fragile before visible collapse? **Research:** dated store, inventory, payables, borrowing, capex and supplier-delivery records; contractual DPO versus arrears; management and governance events. **Article path:** separate company chronologies and bounded cash bridges, then a like-for-like comparison. Establish whether stop-supply, empty shelves, closures and governance decisions preceded or followed liquidity stress. Rate what a manager, supplier, lender and outside analyst could actually know at each date. **Workbook modules:** `Case Register`, `Timeline` and `Evidence Ledger` preserve separate legal records; `Retail Review` is a fictional store contribution and cash-cycle template. **Evidence gate:** verify store counts, revenue, stock shortfalls and causal claims in primary materials; do not invent historical owner earnings where records are incomplete.

### Part 13 — Twiga, a verified startup comparator and cross-case warnings

**Decision:** Did external capital cover a weak operating cash engine, and which warning signals could have prompted action before runway ran out? **Research:** Twiga's verified legal/operating status by date; inventory ownership, spoilage, customer credit, supplier terms, infrastructure, unit contribution, funding rounds and governance. Copia remains a comparator for documented legal records, not an asserted direct operating peer. **Article path:** dated case timelines, confidence-tagged cash/runway boundaries and a comparative warning matrix across the startup cases, Nakumatt and Tuskys. Include owner earnings, contribution, DSO, DIO, DPO, overdue payables, debt service, runway, governance overrides, supplier response and customer collection; rate each signal's contemporaneous visibility, false-positive risk and practical response. September 2026 reporting identifies GT Flow Limited (formerly Twiga Foods One Limited) and Templar Field Limited (formerly Twiga Foods Limited) as under administration; the official notices remain a publication check. **Workbook modules:** `Case Register`, `Timeline`, `Evidence Ledger`, `Funding`, `Cash and CCC`, and `Warnings and Actions` preserve entity distinctions and keep company cash figures unavailable. **Evidence gate:** confirm legal status, funding totals, exchange conversions and causal claims from primary evidence before publication.
