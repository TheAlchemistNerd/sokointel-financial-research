## Verification gates before publication or workbook use

These files preserve prior research, not verified financial or historical facts. The rough Word file includes many secondary links and conversational claims. Recheck Buffett quotations against Berkshire's original letters; company values against dated SEC filings, issuer reports and footnotes; regulatory claims against court or agency material; and thresholds against the relevant sector. Neither the source's 40% gross-margin screen nor its 50%-of-net-income capex screen is a universal rule.

The organised R Markdown's illustrative owner-earnings table needs correction before reuse. Adding its listed rows produces 510, 555 and 603 ($M), while the printed result row says 520, 587 and 643 ($M). The table also needs an explicit stock-based-compensation convention: adding SBC back in operating cash flow and then deducting its economic cost can be appropriate, but deducting an expense already included in net income a second time would be wrong. Preserve the raw example here; build a reconciled, independently checked model in a future workbook.

The phrase that strong coverage makes default risk "effectively zero" is too strong for publication. Coverage is one input among liquidity, debt maturity, covenants, legal claims, operating shocks and refinancing risk. Likewise, blanket claims about Buffett's view of Merton and company-specific regulatory effects need primary-source verification and measured wording. The manuscript plan already treats search cards and AI-generated assertions as leads rather than authority.
