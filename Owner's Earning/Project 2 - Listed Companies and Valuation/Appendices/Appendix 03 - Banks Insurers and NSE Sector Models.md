# Appendix 03 — Regulated financial companies and sector-specific cash questions

### Companion to Project 2, Parts 9 and 10

Banks, insurers and operating companies do not have the same owner-earnings bridge. Deposits and insurance premiums are operating liabilities; regulatory capital and claims-paying resources constrain distributions. In other sectors, the key reinvestment may be a power plant, a crop cycle, a network or property held for sale. The formulas here are model structures, not substitutes for current law, regulator returns or issuer filings.

## A3.1 A bank's distributable-capital test

A bank earns a spread by funding assets with deposits, wholesale liabilities and equity. The analyst should separate pre-provision operating earnings from expected credit losses, realized write-offs, funding costs, tax and capital needs. A rising loan book may increase future earnings and current capital consumption at the same time.

A useful starting framework is:

`Normalized common earnings = normalized net interest income + sustainable fee income − operating costs − through-cycle credit losses − tax`.

Then estimate what can be distributed only after testing:

`Excess distributable capital = eligible capital − binding regulatory minimum − management/board buffer − stress loss requirement`.

This arithmetic must be applied to the correct consolidated or subsidiary perimeter and current capital definition. Liquidity, legal distributable reserves, supervisory restrictions, asset encumbrance and the board's dividend proposal may be more binding than a simple capital ratio. Do not treat all cash or retained earnings as distributable. Stress loan growth, non-performing loans, provisioning, collateral values, deposit concentration and rate changes, and reconcile each stress to capital and cash.

A bank residual-income or dividend model should use the required return on equity and book value with explicit retention and loss assumptions. Barth's peer-reviewed evidence on investment securities and bank valuation illustrates that accounting measurement of securities can affect the way equity markets value banks [1]; it does not supply a Kenyan bank's current fair-value adjustment or capital requirement.

## A3.2 An insurer's earnings and claims-paying capacity

Premium receipts are not immediately free cash: coverage creates future claims, expenses, reinsurance settlements and capital requirements. Under IFRS 17, insurance revenue, insurance service result, risk adjustment, contractual service margin and finance effects should be read with the notes and the group's measurement approach. Compare underwriting performance with investment results and prior-year development of claims reserves. A high reported profit or dividend does not alone establish that regulated subsidiaries can upstream cash.

A practical insurer distribution bridge is:

`Parent cash distributable = parent cash and permitted subsidiary remittances − parent obligations − required local capital and liquidity buffers − expected claim and expense needs`.

Test each subsidiary separately where regulation, policyholder funds or currency controls apply. Reconcile group profit to profit attributable to ordinary holders, dividends actually received at the parent, non-controlling interests and proposed dividends. Cummins' analysis of capital allocation in insurance emphasizes the role of capital in a risk-bearing business [2]; it is a conceptual guide, not a statutory solvency formula for Jubilee.

Jubilee's FY2025 announcement described an 18% increase in net profit and a record dividend; its H1 2026 release reported profit before tax of KES 4.4bn, up 30% [3], [4]. These are differently dated issuer-reported measures. H1 profit before tax cannot be annualized into full-year profit attributable to ordinary shareholders without seasonality, tax, non-controlling interest, claims and investment-result analysis. Before any payout conclusion, reconcile annual and half-year statements, parent cash, segment results, regulated capital and dividend dates. The present workbook does not calculate Jubilee's solvency margin or a complete distributable-capital estimate.

## A3.3 Seven issuers, seven evidence paths

Part 10's companies can be placed on one page for triage, then analyzed with sector-specific models. The primary reports listed in that article are the source perimeter. The table records what to calculate next; it does not imply the workbooks contain completed owner-earnings valuations for all seven.

| Issuer | Primary cash question | Core schedules to build | Important unresolved items |
|---|---|---|---|
| Equity Group | How much earnings growth survives credit losses and capital retention? | Multi-year net interest and fee bridge; ECL and NPL roll-forward; capital and payout | Geographic and subsidiary capital, through-cycle loss rates, parent remittances |
| Jubilee Holdings | What portion of reported group profit and dividend is repeatable and upstreamable? | Insurance service result; claims development; investment result; solvency by regulated entity | Subsidiary capital, claim reserve development, parent liquidity and dividend cover |
| KenGen | What cash remains after maintaining generation capacity and funding construction? | CFO to capex bridge; plant additions and disposals; output, fuel/hydrology and PPA economics | Maintenance versus expansion allocation, project timing, tariff and offtake exposure |
| Kakuzi | How do biological production cycles, crop mix and shipment timing affect cash? | Crop and segment revenue; biological-asset changes; inventory, receivables and capex | Seasonality, weather, yield, land use, commodity and export market exposure |
| Safaricom | Which network and market-entry outlays preserve current service versus add growth? | Service revenue; segment margins; network and spectrum spending; lease and subsidiary cash | Maintenance split, Ethiopia ramp, mobile-money regulation and intercompany flows |
| BAT Kenya | How do volume, price, excise/tax and working capital translate into shareholder cash? | Net sales and tax bridge; volume/mix; CFO; asset replacement; dividend | Maintenance evidence, regulatory change, future volume and product mix |
| Home Afrika | When do property contracts become collected cash and what capital remains tied in projects? | Project inventory/WIP; deposits, completions, receivables; debt and disposal schedule | Project-level margins, contract cancellation, restricted customer funds and liquidity |

Use the same output labels across issuers—reported cash, normalized owner-earnings range, distributable capital and value per share—but do not force the same formula. Compare ranges only after matching dates, currency, claims and evidence quality. The evidence matrix in the workbook is a source map and a triage tool, not seven complete sector models.

## A3.4 Publication gate

A named issuer estimate should not be published until the calculation can be reproduced from primary reports. Preserve statement pages, notes, fiscal periods, exact units, downloaded date and formulas. Keep issuer guidance separate from analyst assumptions. A missing input can be marked “not disclosed” or “not yet verified.” A model that leaves the output blank until evidence arrives is more useful than one that fills the blank with a sector average.
