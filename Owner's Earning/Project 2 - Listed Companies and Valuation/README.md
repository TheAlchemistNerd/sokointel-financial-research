## How to use this folder

Status: five article drafts and the project workbook are built as of 24 September 2026. The workbook includes one filing-led NVIDIA bridge, an Equinix asset-heavy screen, a simplified bank-capital illustration, a seven-issuer NSE evidence matrix and nine normalized profile-specific stress scenarios (two US and seven NSE). It still does not contain a complete valuation or distributable-capital model for every issuer. This README follows the [canonical three-project, thirteen-part programme](../Canonical%20Research%20Programme%20-%20Three%20Projects%20and%20Thirteen%20Parts.md).

Blue cells identify editable assumptions; red cells identify sourced values; formulas and cross-sheet links remain visible. The `Profile Stress` sheet applies analyst-selected shocks to normalized indices; its outputs are explanatory scenarios, not issuer forecasts or cross-company value comparisons. The NVIDIA valuation stays unavailable until required earnings, growth, discount, share-count and price inputs are supported. Sources and Checks contains workbook reconciliations. `Evidence Vectors` carries each populated issuer or regulator measure with its entity, amount/unit, fiscal period, event date, source-record date, publication/filing date, retrieval date, source ID, worksheet locator, evidence state and limits. Scenario rows are separately identified; dates and source page locators that were not captured stay explicitly unknown. Formulas are not protected, and separate change-log or verification-case tabs are not included.

The shared owner-earnings calculation begins with reconciled profit, adds justified non-cash charges, and deducts maintenance investment and required additional operating working capital. Owner compensation, taxes, debt claims, liquidity reserves and sector-specific capital needs require further treatment. Period cash, normalized owner earnings and prudent distributions will be shown separately. The complete calculation and sector adjustments remain in the canonical programme.

## Part-by-part research and workbook breakdown

### Part 6 — Reconstructing owner earnings from public filings

**Decision:** What dated per-share cash-earning range can an investor defend? **Research:** income/cash-flow/balance-sheet links, non-cash items, maintenance versus growth capex, working-capital versus financing movements, claim type and share-count change. **Article path:** rebuild multi-year filing history, normalize cycle and one-offs, show low/base/high owner earnings, then introduce equity value, enterprise-to-equity and reverse-DCF checks without claiming precision. **Workbook modules:** `Filing Inputs`, `NVDA Bridge`, `Assumptions`, `Valuation` and `Sources and Checks` demonstrate the bridge using NVIDIA FY2026. It is not a multi-year filing model for every issuer. **Evidence gate:** use audited reports and a dated price/share count for an issuer-specific result.

### Part 7 — US technology and asset-light economics, anchored by NVIDIA

**Decision:** What remains for owners after physical and intangible productive capacity is maintained? **Research:** NVIDIA's reported cash bridge, cyclicality, inventory, supply commitments and customer concentration; R&D and capitalized software; SBC, dilution/buybacks and deferred obligations. **Article path:** a dated NVIDIA filing reconstruction, then a selected software/platform comparison showing different asset and revenue economics. Stress demand, gross margin, reinvestment, working capital and share count. **Workbook modules:** `Filing Inputs`, `Assumptions`, `NVDA Bridge` and `Valuation` preserve reported values and selectable SBC treatment while leaving maintenance, normalized working capital, diluted shares and market price blank where evidence is required. `Profile Stress` adds the article's hypothetical semiconductor demand/inventory scenario. **Evidence gate:** use current SEC and issuer filings before a numerical claim.

### Part 8 — Asset-heavy issuers and data-centre infrastructure

**Decision:** Which cash spending preserves existing service and which creates new capacity? **Research:** physical replacement cycles, power/cooling, real estate, leases, financing and construction-in-progress; unit economics of an established asset-heavy issuer and a selected data-centre infrastructure issuer. **Article path:** reconcile total capex into maintenance, compliance and growth ranges; model debt/lease claims, utilisation and customer concentration. Add an energy-transition or regulated-utility sidebar to contrast capital intensity under different revenue protections. **Workbook modules:** `Asset Heavy` separates Equinix filed property, depreciation and issuer-defined recurring capex from a fictional KES project contribution/yield screen; `Profile Stress` shows a separate utilization/power and renewal scenario on a normalized index, not an Equinix forecast. **Evidence gate:** use audited filings, capex/lease notes and project records for issuer estimates.

### Part 9 — Banks and insurers: distributable capital

**Decision:** What can a financial institution distribute while remaining able to lend, absorb losses and pay claims? **Research:** bank loan growth, expected credit losses, funding and regulatory capital; insurer underwriting, claims reserves, investments, asset-liability matching and solvency. Explain why ordinary inventory/CCC and physical-capex formulas cannot simply be transferred to either institution. **Article path:** a bank normalized-earnings/excess-capital/residual-income bridge and an insurer underwriting/reserving/solvency bridge, each with stress cases and payout limits. **Workbook modules:** `Bank Insurance` separates CBK system ratios and Jubilee group results from a simplified bank-capital illustration; `Profile Stress` adds distinct normalized credit-loss/retention and claims/reserve shocks, but does not calculate issuer-level insurer solvency or bank credit stress. **Evidence gate:** use audited reports, regulator returns and current rules for issuer-specific payout tests.

### Part 10 — NSE cross-sector casebook and decision framework

**Decision:** How should Kenyan issuers be compared without one formula flattening their economics? **Research scope:** actual dated cases across **NSE banks, insurance, energy, agriculture, telecommunications, manufacturing and property**. The bank and insurer methods extend Part 9; non-financial cases apply the correct replacement, lease, commodity, biological-asset, spectrum, network, capacity, property and fair-value adjustments. **Article path:** a source-linked bridge and low/base/high range per selected issuer, plus a cross-sector matrix of cash claim, adjustment sensitivity, governance, currency, state influence, liquidity and evidence quality. Compare the estimate with dividends and price at a defined date; no forced ranking by a single ratio. **Workbook modules:** `NSE Casebook` records selected metrics and source IDs for seven issuers; the KenGen capital-investment-to-PAT ratio is calculated. `Profile Stress` pairs those seven NSE profiles with NVIDIA and Equinix under separate normalized downside assumptions. It is a scenario teaching tool, not seven complete sector valuations. **Evidence gate:** use NSE/CMA, issuer and sector-regulator primary records before extending the model.
