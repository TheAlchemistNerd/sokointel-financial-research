# Listed Companies and Valuation — Video Tutorial Guide

Updated 24 September 2026. This production guide covers five stand-alone episodes based on Parts 6–10. It is a video plan, not a recorded script, finished deck or published video.

## Audience, voice and format

The audience is a public-market investor, analyst or investment-committee member who wants to move from filed statements to a defensible owner-earnings range and decision. Speak as an analyst tracing a claim from source document to calculation. Keep the reported number, analyst estimate, forecast and valuation output visibly separate.

Plan 16–21 minutes per episode. Put the company, reporting entity, fiscal period, currency, unit and filing date beside each sourced figure. The Project 2 workbook has two kinds of demonstrations: dated issuer facts and transparent analyst scenarios. Do not fill an unavailable maintenance, working-capital or per-share input with zero merely to complete a valuation.

## Course trailer

Use a 60–90 second trailer to show the sequence: reconstruct a filed cash bridge; distinguish reinvestment from maintenance; test technology and asset-heavy businesses; analyze bank and insurer capital; and compare seven NSE sectors using their own reporting periods. Preview both U.S. and NSE profile stresses. State that the model makes assumptions inspectable; it does not turn missing evidence into an intrinsic value.

## Production and evidence rules

- Use filed company numbers with the company, reporting entity, currency, unit, fiscal period and filing date on the slide. A system aggregate must not be presented as an issuer measure.
- Use the article’s peer-reviewed accounting and valuation research in the episode notes where it informs a method. State the study’s question and sample; do not imply that the research supplies a maintenance number for these issuers.
- Do not invent the unreported maintenance/NWC split for NVIDIA, infer complete maintenance from Equinix recurring capex, or turn a simplified bank proxy into regulatory headroom.
- In Profile Stress, label both US cases and all seven NSE cases as analyst-designed stresses. The residual index is not a valuation multiple, expected return or issuer forecast.
- Keep issuer facts, article hypothetical examples and workbook scenario inputs on separate visual lanes. Show source IDs from Sources and Checks and source locators from Evidence Vectors in speaker notes.
- Use the relevant appendices for formulas, sensitivity tables and full references. Build charts from the saved workbook data with periods and units intact.
