## 5. Evidence hierarchy and claim labels

Every material case claim should be tagged in the article source notes or future ledger as one of:

1. **Filed/official fact:** audited company filing, regulator, statute, Gazette, court judgment, administrator report, or dated official notice. Preserve reporting period and legal entity.
2. **Peer-reviewed evidence:** published journal article. State its sample, period, setting, and measured outcome before applying it. A finding in Belgian large-company data is not a Kenya-wide MSME law.
3. **Official guidance or accounting rule:** useful for classification and definitions, not proof that a company performed well.
4. **Company or administrator statement:** attributed to the speaker and date; it is not independently verified just because it appears in a press release.
5. **Secondary reporting:** useful to find documents and reconstruct chronology. Attribute it. Do not use it to prove a disputed number where primary records should exist.
6. **Analyst calculation:** reproduce the formula, source values, period, currency, and assumption. Reconcile it to the filing.
7. **Illustration or hypothesis:** mark it as fictional/hypothetical in the text and workbook. Do not let its result appear to be a Kenyan market average.
8. **Unresolved:** state the exact missing evidence and what conclusion must wait.

Use IEEE-style numbered references within each article. Put citations next to the sentence or exhibit they support, not only in a closing bibliography. Give a durable DOI, issuer filing, official court, or regulator URL wherever one exists. A news link can document who reported a claim and when; it cannot become the sole support for a causal conclusion.

## 6. Calculation rules for this programme

### 6.1 Four different cash questions

Do not use these labels interchangeably:

* **Cash generated this period:** reconciled operating receipts and payments over the stated historical period.
* **Owner earnings estimate:** an analytical estimate of recurring earning power after the capital spending and incremental working capital needed to maintain unit volume and competitive position. It is not cash in the bank or a guaranteed dividend.
* **Prudent distributable cash:** cash left after near-term obligations, tax, debt covenants, restricted funds, required reserves, and the owner’s chosen resilience buffer.
* **Value to equity owners:** a valuation question about future equity cash flow or residual income, discount rates, and risk. A balance-sheet cash amount or trailing owner-earnings estimate alone does not answer it.

For a simplified period bridge, show operating profit or net income, tax and interest treatment, non-cash charges, working-capital movements, cash capital spending, scheduled debt service, restrictions, and proposed distributions. Label the bridge as a model and align every line to the same entity, currency, and period.

### 6.2 Maintenance investment is not a fixed fraction

Use Berkshire’s 1986 explanation as the source for the owner-earnings term. Its maintenance-capital estimate asks what a business needs to spend to keep long-term competitive position and unit volume, then adds incremental working capital needed for that same purpose. The source itself warns that this estimate can be difficult. Depreciation, total capital expenditure, management guidance, physical condition, capacity additions, and replacement cycles are clues, not interchangeable answers. Show an evidenced range or “not estimable from disclosures”; never insert a generic 30%, 50%, or 100% of D&A without issuer-specific support.

### 6.3 Working capital and debt claims

Show receivables, inventory, payables, contract assets, and customer advances by source and date. A negative cash-conversion cycle is not automatically safe: it can depend on supplier terms, refunds, service quality, or rapid growth. Distinguish EBITDA, EBIT, CFO, free cash flow, debt capacity, CFADS, and cash left for the shareholder. A loan draw raises cash and debt together; it is financing, not a customer receipt or profit.

Facility pricing, fees, penalties, repayment dates, eligible invoices, and collateral come from the actual contract. DSCR or CFADS is not a universal legal covenant definition. Show a calculation using named assumptions and identify the contract clause that controls any real borrowing decision.

### 6.4 Stock-based compensation and owner dilution

Stock-based compensation is not “free” simply because it is noncash in the current-period cash flow statement. It is also not an excuse to subtract the same expense twice. State the selected approach: (a) use earnings after the reported compensation expense and do not add it back; or (b) add it back to cash flow and separately model the resulting dilution or replacement cost. Keep grants, vesting, exercises, repurchases, and diluted share count tied to the correct dates and disclosures.

### 6.5 Valuation is not precision theatre

Use a sector-appropriate model, show a range, and name the two or three assumptions that move the answer most. For financial companies, model regulatory/solvency capital, reserves, asset and liability quality, and claims rather than substituting industrial-company net debt and capex conventions. For a distressed company, distinguish going-concern value, restructuring value, secured recovery, creditor recovery, and shareholder residual value.

## 7. Article production and evidence gates

Each part should include: a reader-facing opening; plain-language definitions; a worked but explicitly hypothetical example when useful; a practical decision process; common failure modes; a short workbook map; and numbered references. Reported data must show fiscal or calendar period, publication date, currency and entity. Do not represent estimates as audited figures.

Article names follow the Part 01–13 titles in the canonical programme. Article 10 is a bounded seven-sector casebook, not a recommendation list. Article 12 bases Nakumatt and Tuskys claims on court and administrator records. Article 13 compares the specific Twiga/GT Flow or Templar Field legal entity with Copia only for the dimensions supported by documents; do not combine either administration with every affiliate. Administration is not liquidation, and a fundraising total is not revenue, cash remaining, or creditor loss.

For failures, preserve two clocks: what the market or lenders could see at each past date, and what became known later through litigation or administration. Mark hindsight-only disclosures. Make causal language proportional to the evidence: “the judgment records”, “the administrator reported”, “the company announced”, “contemporaneous filings show”, or “we cannot determine from this record”.

### 7.1 Word-count, workbook and appendix consistency

Each of the sixteen current article drafts has at least 3,500 body words before references. Revisions must preserve the body length of any article already above 3,500 words; do not shorten an article to fit an appendix, workbook note or style pass. Additions should improve analysis or traceability rather than repeat filler.

For every worked example, match the company or fictional-case label, period, unit, input values, formula and output in the article, appendix and workbook. If an article uses a second hypothetical example, call it a separate example and do not imply the workbook calculates it. Each article map should identify exact sheet names and what the workbook does not establish. The project appendix index should link each appendix to the article and workbook sheets it supports. A formula check can verify arithmetic; it cannot validate unsupported inputs or prove a scenario is likely.
