# What Startup Funding Figures Actually Measure

This folder holds the source capture and **research plan** for a proposed three-part SokoIntel companion series. The user-supplied LinkedIn screenshots prompted the work; they are preserved, transcribed and treated as claims to test. No articles, workbook or blog catalog entries have been published from this plan.

1. [Screenshot source capture](01%20-%20Screenshot%20Source%20Capture.md) records all three images, the visible funding amounts, their Sh93.31 billion arithmetic sum and the verification limits.
2. [Funding figures analysis and three-part programme](02%20-%20Funding%20Figures%20Analysis%20and%20Three%20Part%20Programme.md) explains the 2025/H1 2026 comparison, equity and debt, concentration, outcome terminology, sources, three article outlines and a future interactive workbook.
3. [Claim and company verification ledger](03%20-%20Claim%20and%20Company%20Verification%20Ledger.md) separates supported tracker figures, derived arithmetic, disputed interpretations and remaining evidence work for all 13 companies.
4. [Original screenshot copies](Sources/Screenshots/) preserve the supplied evidence. Copy hashes appear in the source capture.

This series studies the **funding market**. The adjacent [Owner Earnings thirteen-part programme](../Canonical%20Research%20Programme%20-%20Three%20Projects%20and%20Thirteen%20Parts.md) studies **company-level cash earning power** and uses funding histories in Parts 11-13. A company can raise a great deal and still have poor cash economics; a funding downturn can also coexist with stronger underwriting. Those hypotheses require dated transaction and operating evidence, not a headline total.

Before publication, obtain the underlying deal ledger and original company/administrator documents, resolve country attribution and instrument classification, recreate all concentration figures, and test company outcomes individually. The screenshots and any social summaries are leads, not verified financial data.
