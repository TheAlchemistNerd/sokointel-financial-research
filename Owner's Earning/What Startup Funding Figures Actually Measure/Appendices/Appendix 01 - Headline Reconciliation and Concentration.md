# Appendix 01 — Headline reconciliation and concentration

### Companion to Part 01

This appendix reproduces the arithmetic in the [Startup Funding Market and Outcomes workbook](../Startup%20Funding%20Market%20and%20Outcomes%20Workbook.xlsx). It distinguishes three kinds of information: tracker-reported observations, figures transcribed from the supplied screenshots, and calculations from those inputs. A calculation can be exact while its underlying input remains rounded or unverified.

## A1.1 The full-year headline and its categories

The ATBD year-end post published on 13 January 2026 describes Kenya as having raised almost US$1 billion in calendar 2025, and publishes US$582 million of debt and US$383 million of equity [1]. It does not give an exact US$984 million total in that post. Contemporary press coverage published on 15 and 19 January attributes the exact US$984 million figure to ATBD [6–8]. The workbook therefore records $984 million as a contemporary secondary-reported ATBD total, not merely as a screenshot claim; it also preserves the provider's primary wording as rounded. Report and press publication dates do not establish the underlying transaction cutoff or data vintage. See Appendix 04 for the full reporting-vector comparison.

The tracker reports US$582 million of debt and US$383 million of equity. Their category subtotal is:

`US$582m debt + US$383m equity = US$965m`

Measured against that category subtotal, the arithmetic shares are:

| Calculation | Result | Denominator |
|---|---:|---|
| Debt share | 582 / 965 = 60.31% | Reported debt plus reported equity |
| Equity share | 383 / 965 = 39.69% | Reported debt plus reported equity |
| Difference from exact secondary-reported total | 984 − 965 = US$19m | Contemporary reports' ATBD-attributed total less category subtotal |

The US$19 million is an unresolved difference. It might reflect different source versions, rounded totals, other instruments, timing, coverage or a transcription difference. The inspected reports do not provide a transaction-level bridge or a dated snapshot that identifies the cause. The available information does not justify assigning it to grants or treating the two totals as if they shared an exactly reconciled denominator. The workbook's `Funding Mix` sheet keeps the inputs in separate rows and labels the difference rather than plugging it.

This also corrects the loose statement that “60% was debt” when the visible base is unclear. Debt is about 60.3% of debt plus equity. If a reader divides US$582 million by the screenshot's US$984 million, the result is 59.1%. Both ratios can be calculated, but only the first is the debt share of the specified tracker category subtotal; neither should be presented without its denominator.

## A1.2 Growth and reporting-date check

ATBD's primary Kenya summary was published on 13 January 2026 and says “almost” US$1bn for calendar 2025. Exact US$984m reporting was published in the inspected press sources on 15 January (The Star) and 19 January (Business Daily and The EastAfrican). Partech released its approximately US$1.04bn report on 22 January. The publication dates are therefore nine days apart for the two provider reports and seven days apart between the inspected exact ATBD report and Partech's report. They do not identify the underlying data cutoffs or transactions each provider had captured.

Using the exact secondary-reported 2025 total and the official 2024 report's US$638m figure:

`(US$984m / US$638m) − 1 = 54.23%`, or about **54.2%**.

That is 2.23 percentage points above ATBD's stated +52%. If US$984m and +52% referred to an identical unrounded series, the arithmetic prior-year base would be `984 / 1.52 = US$647.37m`, US$9.37m above the published US$638m. This is a diagnostic calculation, not evidence that ATBD revised its 2024 figure. Rounding or a vintage difference may explain it, but the inspected sources do not resolve the discrepancy. ATBD's May 2026 note says deals can be disclosed with delay; it does not report a revision to the Kenya 2025 total. Appendix 04 records the full report-level and transaction-level fields that would be needed to test that hypothesis.

## A1.3 Matched half-year comparison

The tracker reports Kenya funding of US$227 million in H1 2025 and US$126 million in H1 2026. The matched-period change is:

`(US$126m / US$227m) − 1 = −44.4934%`, or approximately **−44.5%**.

These are rounded published totals. The result is a descriptive comparison under the tracker coverage, not proof of why funding changed. It does not compare H1 2026 with the whole of 2025. The same H1 2026 report attributes US$46 million of equity to Kenya, against US$214 million in Nigeria and US$183 million in Egypt. Those are tracker-defined national aggregates; they do not alone measure investor confidence across all company stages, founder groups or deal sizes.

The 2025 tracker also reports 75 Kenyan ventures raising at least US$100,000, down 23% year over year. The threshold matters. It is a count of ventures clearing a defined funding threshold, not necessarily all startup deals, rounds or first-time recipients. A broader analysis would retain venture count, round count, median amount, stage, repeat access and instrument as distinct measures.

## A1.4 Screenshot concentration arithmetic

The third supplied screenshot lists these KES billion claims:

| Company as displayed | Screenshot amount (KES bn) |
|---|---:|
| Twiga Foods | 24.10 |
| Copia | 16.00 |
| Gro Intelligence | 15.30 |
| Koko Networks | 13.00 |
| Mobius Motors | 7.30 |
| MarketForce | 5.50 |
| Wefarm | 4.20 |
| Sendy | 3.20 |
| iProcure | 2.20 |
| Lipa Later | 2.20 |
| Kune Foods | 0.13 |
| Zumi | 0.13 |
| Notify Logistics | 0.05 |
| **Displayed amount sum** | **93.31** |

The largest five listed values sum to KES75.70 billion. Their share of the screenshot's selected list is:

`KES75.70bn / KES93.31bn = 81.13%`

This calculation reproduces concentration **within those 13 selected screenshot entries**. It is not the 2025 Kenya funding-market concentration, and it does not validate the list's currencies, original deal years, instrument mix, exchange rates, legal recipients, drawn amounts or cash receipts. It cannot support the statement that five companies received 81% or 82% of total Kenya startup funding.

A separate tracker sector article reports an 82% share for five large energy companies across Africa. That statistic has a different geography and sector denominator. Its similarity to the screenshot percentage does not make the values interchangeable. A country-specific concentration result would need a complete Kenya deal ledger, an explicit rule for regional companies and a common funding-period cutoff.

## A1.5 Workbook reproduction

In `Market Metrics`, edit the displayed tracker observations only when a new source version is acquired; record the source date and coverage before replacing them. The H1 change is calculated from the two half-year inputs. In `Funding Mix`, debt and equity are summed and the screenshot difference is left visible. In `Screenshot List`, the 13 amounts sum to KES93.31 billion; rows 6–10 calculate the top-five KES75.70 billion and 81.13% selected-list share. `Sources and Checks` tests those calculations against the displayed values.

The workbook's blue inputs do not certify source quality. If the underlying deal ledger is unavailable, the proper result is a transparent reproduction of a reported or screenshot claim with an evidence limitation—not a fabricated list of transactions. The `Reporting Vectors` sheet records the report dates and shows the nine-day interval as publication timing only; transaction cutoffs and vintages remain unknown.
