# What Startup Funding Figures Actually Measure

Research analysis and editorial blueprint, first drafted 22 September 2026. This file records the source definitions and editorial plan that informed the completed draft series; it is not itself the article set or workbook. The supplied LinkedIn screenshots are captured in [01 - Screenshot Source Capture.md](01%20-%20Screenshot%20Source%20Capture.md); their claims remain research leads, not independently verified transactions.

## Editorial decision

Give the question its own three-part series while retaining firm-level funding histories in Parts 11-13 of the [Owner Earnings thirteen-part programme](../Canonical%20Research%20Programme%20-%20Three%20Projects%20and%20Thirteen%20Parts.md). The unit of analysis differs. This series asks what a national startup-funding headline reveals about access to capital and the structure of financing. Owner Earnings asks whether a particular enterprise generated enough cash to maintain operations, honour claims and survive stress. The two projects should link directly, not repeat each other.

The central reader question is: when reported funding drops, has investor appetite weakened, have investors become more selective, or has a prior period simply contained unusually large facilities? A headline total alone cannot distinguish these explanations.

The screenshot's proposed explanation has merit, but its causal claims go beyond these facts. The primary tracker identifies d.light, Sun King, M-Kopa, Burn and PowerGen as major drivers of Kenya's 2025 amount [1]. It does not, in the public country account inspected, publish a verified 82% share for those five firms. A separate tracker analysis says the top five **energy companies across Africa** represented US$701 million, or 82% of the **continent-wide energy-sector** total [4]. Those are different denominators. Do not reproduce “82% of Kenya's funding went to five companies” until a deal-level Kenya ledger recreates it under a stated country-allocation method.

Country allocation itself is consequential. AVCA notes that several Kenya-headquartered but multi-region startups would add US$761 million to its East Africa total if assigned solely to the region [5]. Divergent totals across trackers can therefore reflect different coverage, company geography, funding instruments, deal thresholds and announcement dates. A comparison must identify its dataset before interpreting the rank.

## What debt and equity actually say

Debt is not inherently weak funding. A receivables-backed facility can be suitable when a firm has verifiable instalment collections and uses proceeds to finance customer assets. It can signal lender confidence in a specific pool of assets, while leaving equity holders exposed to losses after debt claims. Equity can finance uncertain growth without scheduled repayment, but a large equity round does not prove that unit economics or owner earnings are sound. The research should map each instrument to its obligor, security, cost, maturity, currency, drawdown terms and operating cash source. Sun King's disclosed securitisation and working-capital facilities illustrate why the label “debt” requires further analysis [6].

The basic capital-quality test is: what activity is funded, what cash claim does it create, when must it be paid, and who bears a shortfall? For an owner or lender, this connects to the [business-credit series](../../African%20Markets%20and%20Business%20Credit/LinkedIn%20Articles/Part%202%20-%20Business%20Credit%20and%20Fintech.md). For the Owner Earnings programme it connects to Part 3's cash forecast, Part 4's debt timing, and Part 11's retrospective funding register.

## Reading the 13-company screenshot responsibly

The amounts in screenshot 03 add to Sh93.31 billion, close to its rounded Sh93 billion claim. This arithmetic check proves neither that every amount was disbursed nor that Sh93 billion was lost. Announced commitments, drawn facilities, equity, grants and later refinancing must be separated. A lender may recover part of a loan; an investor may have sold an interest; a business in administration may continue trading; and a founder's legal entity, operating subsidiary and group may have different outcomes. Currency conversions must be dated and the original USD amount retained.

The screenshot groups all 13 companies under “collapsed.” That label is too coarse for a rigorous casebook. A company-level ledger should record **operating**, **downsized**, **restructured**, **under administration**, **liquidated**, **acquired**, **closed**, or **unresolved**, with a date, exact entity name and underlying document. The funded entity may differ from the Kenyan operator or brand. A separate source, FSD Kenya, earlier distinguished firms that closed, entered administration or exited Kenya from firms, including Twiga at the time, that downsized or restructured [7]. Its funding total for Twiga differs from the screenshot, further showing why the source date and counting rule matter.

As of September 2026, reporting identifies GT Flow Limited (formerly Twiga Foods One Limited) and Templar Field Limited (formerly Twiga Foods Limited) as under administration [8]. Kenya's Business Registration Service calls administration an alternative to liquidation [9]. The contemporaneous Kenya Gazette notice and administrator materials should be acquired for publication. The outcome for each legal entity, creditors and ongoing operations remains a separate question. Do not assert that every shilling raised was lost or that administration proves the whole group has permanently ceased business.

## The question cannot be resolved by funding totals alone

**Evidence compatible with deterioration:** fewer first-time and smaller tickets, lower follow-on rates, viable firms unable to refinance, worsening collections, rising distressed restructurings, or a sustained fall after controlling for megadeals.

**Evidence compatible with healthy selection:** funding of businesses with stronger retention, contribution margins, repayment performance, cash conversion and survival, even if fewer deals close. A higher share of investors demanding such proof is a hypothesis until surveys, deal terms or investor communications support it.

Both processes may occur at once. A market can become more selective because investors perceive real risk while sound small firms still face a financing gap. The series should show the reader what additional evidence would distinguish these accounts rather than pronounce a national verdict from one six-month total. A lower equity total is not automatically a more “honest” confidence gauge than debt; each answers a different financing question.

## Three-part article programme

### Part 1 — The headline and its denominator

**Reader decision:** Is the funding market broadening or being carried by a handful of transactions? **Research questions:** What counts as a startup, deal, country and dollar raised? How do thresholds, reported versus confidential deals, geography and full-year versus half-year timing change rankings? **Worked displays:** H1 2025/H1 2026 matched comparison; deal count alongside dollars; total excluding the largest one and five deals; company- and deal-level concentration, with source-specific denominators. **Evidence gate:** reproduce every percentage from the source deal ledger; if underlying transactions are unavailable, label concentration as reported and unverified. **Approximate article scope:** 3,500 words.

### Part 2 — What kind of money was raised

**Reader decision:** Does the financing instrument fit the asset and repayment cycle? **Research questions:** How do equity, venture debt, receivables securitisation, working-capital lines, convertible notes and grants differ? What was committed versus drawn? Which entity owes repayment, in what currency, and from what cash flow? **Worked displays:** equity/debt/grant waterfall; a receivables-backed financing example; debt service under slower collections and FX movement; founder dilution under equity. Use transparent hypothetical values where contracts are not public. **Evidence gate:** original facility, investor and issuer disclosures before attributing deal terms to a named firm. **Approximate article scope:** 3,500 words.

### Part 3 — What happened after the raise

**Reader decision:** Did capital finance a viable cash engine, buy time for one, or conceal weakness? **Research questions:** What happened to unit contribution, customer collections, supplier payments, runway, follow-on funding, governance and legal status after each raise? Which warning was visible at the time rather than only in hindsight? **Worked displays:** dated company funding and operating timelines; side-by-side cases representing different outcomes; sources-to-uses and runway bridge; capital recovery/outcome categories. Twiga and a documented second case can be linked to Owner Earnings Part 13 without reprinting its full owner-earnings model. **Evidence gate:** company, investor, administrator, court and regulator records outrank social summaries; unresolved amounts remain unresolved. **Approximate article scope:** 3,500 words.
