# What Startup Funding Figures Actually Measure — Video Tutorial Guide

Updated 24 September 2026. This guide plans three stand-alone episodes based on the current article series. It is a production guide, not a recorded narration, slide deck or published video.

## Audience, voice and format

The audience is a founder, investor, journalist, policy analyst or business owner who wants to interpret a funding headline and make a sound next decision. Speak as a data-literate colleague: begin with the number people are repeating, then reconstruct what it counts. Explain the denominator, instrument, observation period and source before interpreting the result.

Plan 16–20 minutes per episode. Keep tracker facts, secondary reports, screenshot claims, hypothetical contract examples and company-outcome evidence on separate visual lanes. Put the source, publication date, activity period, currency, unit and disclosure status near each figure. A publication date is not automatically a transaction date or provider cutoff.

## Series trailer

A 60–90 second trailer can preview three questions: how much capital was counted and under which definition; what obligation or ownership claim was raised; and what can be established about the company after the raise? Show the provider comparison, a loan service schedule, a simple dilution calculation and a company evidence register. State that funding aggregates are not company cash balances, market-wide access measures or investor returns.

## Production and evidence rules

- Use current, dated provider, company and legal sources for any fact likely to change before recording. Preserve the 24 September 2026 workbook cutoff in the current guide and update the source ledger before a later recording.
- Keep report date, activity period, announcement/signing/close/draw/receipt date, first-seen date, export snapshot and assigned-year rule separate. A report released nine days after another does not establish a nine-day data cutoff gap.
- Label peer-reviewed studies in Part 2 and Part 3 with their study question, population, period and outcome. A research association is not proof that one instrument caused a Kenyan company’s outcome.
- Use a visible “unknown / not reconciled” state rather than zero for legal recipients, cash actually received, current status or investor recovery when the record does not establish them.
- Put source IDs, publication dates and workbook locations in speaker notes. The appendices carry the full calculation and evidence register; keep the core episode readable.
- Add captions and transcript. Do not present the loan or dilution scenario as an actual term sheet or company financing.
