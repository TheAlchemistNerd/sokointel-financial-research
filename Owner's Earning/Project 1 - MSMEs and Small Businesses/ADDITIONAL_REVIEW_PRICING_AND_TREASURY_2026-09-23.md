## Consistent bridge to use

Start from an explicitly identified earnings measure. For a recurring operating-earnings route, a teaching bridge is:

**normalised EBITDA − cash tax − maintenance capital expenditure − required additional operating working capital = illustrative pre-financing cash available**, subject to the business and covenant definition.

A route starting from net profit or owner earnings requires a different reconciliation so interest, tax, non-cash charges and owner costs are not adjusted twice. SDE commonly reflects an owner-operator perspective; a buyer needing a replacement manager must model that cost. Identify whether the base already includes owner compensation and what labour remains essential.

Then map the result into dated receipts and payments. Deduct debt service when calculating cash after financing. Apply a stated reserve/distribution policy to calculate prudent owner withdrawals. Keep business cash, owner cash and restricted lender cash separate.

The source's project-finance DSCR/LLCR material can teach these distinctions, but legal covenant definitions are facility-specific. Debt-service reserves bridge timing; their drawdown is a financing source rather than additional operating earnings.

### Proposed paragraph for Part 4

Debt capacity has both an amount and a calendar. The business needs recurring cash generation that can support financing over its life, and enough available cash on each payment date. Owner earnings help assess the first question after essential reinvestment. A dated cash forecast answers the second. A business can pass one test and fail the other, particularly when customers pay late, inventory must be bought early or the owner's reported earnings include work that a successor must pay someone else to perform.
