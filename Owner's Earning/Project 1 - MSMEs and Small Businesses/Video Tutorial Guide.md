# Owner Earnings for MSMEs and Small Businesses — Video Tutorial Guide

Updated 24 September 2026. This is a production guide for five stand-alone educational episodes. It is not a recorded narration, finished slide deck or published video.

## Audience, voice and format

The audience is an owner-manager, finance lead, buyer or lender who needs to decide what cash the business can distribute, reinvest, borrow against or transfer. Speak as a practical colleague working through a set of books with the viewer. Start with the operating decision, name the figures and dates, and show how a result follows from its inputs. Define a finance term when it first becomes useful.

Plan 14–18 minutes per episode. Use one fictional business case for the episode’s core calculation and label every invented input. The Part 1 workshop example and the workbook’s general Owner Earnings scenario are separate cases; do not combine their figures. Keep accounting profit, normalized owner earnings, near-term liquidity and a legal distribution decision distinct.

Use the companion workbook as a screen demonstration, not as an unexplained spreadsheet tour. Show a small range with row and column headings, replace one input, follow the formula, then return to a clean slide with the result and its meaning.

## Course trailer

A 60–90 second trailer can follow one question through the series: what must an owner know before taking cash out, choosing a loan, adding capacity or selling the business? Preview the cash bridge, maintenance and working-capital test, 13-week operating routine, financing comparison and succession file. State that the examples are fictional planning cases and that real decisions need reconciled books, contracts and dated operating records.

## Episode plans

### OE1-01 — The cash an owner can actually claim

**Viewer question:** Why can a profitable owner-operated business have little safe cash to draw?  
**Runtime:** 16–18 minutes.  
**Case:** The fictional vehicle-repair workshop in Part 1. Keep the separate, larger Owner Earnings workbook scenario visible only as a second example.

| Time | Screen and teaching beat | Evidence / workbook route |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–1:30 | Open with the owner’s choice: take a draw, finish a customer job, or keep cash for payroll and suppliers. Put profit and bank cash on different cards. | Part 1 opening; define “owner earnings” only after explaining the decision. |
| 1:30–4:00 | Build the workshop bridge: KSh1.8m net income, KSh240k D&A, KSh380k maintenance, KSh370k incremental working capital and KSh300k replacement-pay shortfall. Explain why only the shortfall between current pay and replacement labour is adjusted. | Article Case!C5:F18; Appendix 01, sections A1.1–A1.2. |
| 4:00–6:00 | Separate the KSh300k second-bay expansion from replacement assets. Show why the KSh990k historical cash-after-capex proxy happens to equal normalized owner earnings in this example, but is not a general identity. | Article Case!D14:D18. State that the numerical equality is coincidental. |
| 6:00–9:00 | Work the dated draw test: KSh1.2m bank cash less KSh910k listed uses and a KSh150k reserve leaves KSh140k provisional headroom. Removing a mistakenly counted KSh200k disputed receipt takes the result to negative KSh60k. | Article Case!C21:F33; this is not a legal dividend or solvency test. |
| 9:00–12:00 | Open the workbook’s separate general bridge. It produces KSh2.2m owner earnings and KSh1.2m after its debt and reserve inputs. Explain that this is a different fictional scenario, not the workshop calculation. | Owner Earnings!C5:F22; Overview!C10:D23. The article already distinguishes the two cases. |
| 12:00–16:00 | Give the viewer a one-page task: reconcile a reported period, classify maintenance and working capital, then list obligations by due date. Answer the opening question with the two results: earning power and draw capacity. | Appendix 01 and the article’s monthly owner review. Show cited source titles in notes. |

**On-screen calculation:** KSh1.800m + KSh0.240m − KSh0.380m − KSh0.370m − KSh0.300m = KSh0.990m normalized owner earnings. Keep the growth-bay outlay outside the no-growth bridge.

### OE1-02 — Maintenance capital and working capital

**Viewer question:** What cash investment is required to keep today’s service level, and what cash is tied up in the operating cycle?  
**Runtime:** 15–18 minutes.

| Time | Screen and teaching beat | Evidence / workbook route |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–2:00 | Put depreciation, a replacement quote and growth spending side by side. Ask what job the asset performs and what fails if it is not renewed. | Part 2; Appendix 02. |
| 2:00–5:00 | Build a replacement reserve from asset, age, cost, planning months and evidence still needed. The compressor and van are fictional examples; a quote or inspection would replace the placeholder. | Maintenance!C5:I12, especially D6:I10. |
| 5:00–9:00 | Follow receivables, inventory and payables into DSO, DIO, DPO and the cash-conversion cycle. Show the numerator and denominator rather than treating “days” as a score. | Working Capital!C5:O8. The exported workbook now calculates DSO from receivables/credit sales, DIO from inventory/cost of sales, DPO from payables/credit purchases, and net working capital as receivables + inventory − payables. |
| 9:00–12:00 | Compare the sample months: October DSO 20.0 days, DIO 33.1, DPO 22.1 and CCC 31.0; December CCC is 41.0 days. NWC rises from KSh2.0m to KSh2.9m across the three sample month-ends. | Working Capital!J6:N8. | 12:00–15:00 | Show how a shorter CCC can still be harmful if it comes from missed supplier terms or stock-outs. Build a data request: aged balances, matched credit sales/purchases, customer advances, disputes and seasonal comparison. | Receivables, Inventory and Payables; Appendix 02. |

## Production and evidence rules

- Cite Berkshire’s owner-earnings definition and the peer-reviewed working-capital and replacement-investment studies through the article’s IEEE references. State the study population and setting when drawing on a research result; do not present a study’s association as a universal target.
- Label every fictional model input on screen. The general Owner Earnings worksheet and Article Case tab are different cases. Do not splice their amounts.
- On the Working Capital sheet, retain the matched periods, formula definitions and corrected column mapping. Do not demonstrate an ending balance as an average-balance estimate.
- Put the article, workbook sheet/cell locator, source date and calculation in speaker notes. Use the relevant appendix for full derivations and reference lists.
- Record captions and transcript. Keep the spreadsheet zoomed to one readable range, then return to a clean summary slide. Do not show a real client or counterparty identity.
