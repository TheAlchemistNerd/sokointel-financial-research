## 3. A consistent cash definition

For a simplified **accrual route**:

`pre-financing operating cash ≈ normalized EBITDA − cash tax − maintenance investment − increase in operating working capital ± stated adjustments`.

For a **direct cash route**:

`pre-financing operating cash = customer cash receipts − cash operating payments − cash tax − maintenance payments`.

In the direct route, receipts and payments already reflect the cash consequences of invoices, stock purchases and supplier settlement. Deduct an additional working-capital adjustment only for a specifically identified flow not already represented. If starting with reported CFO, reconcile any normalization by replacing the actual-period movement with the supported normalized movement, rather than deducting both.

Debt proceeds, debt repayment, financing charges and reserves then enter the financing/liquidity bridge according to their dates. State the treatment of interest, leases, customer deposits and tax. Any committed growth spending must also enter the funding calendar. A lender's covenant definition of cash available for debt service may differ from this teaching definition and should be recorded separately.

If calculating debt-service coverage, align numerator and denominator periods and follow the contractual definition. Opening cash or a reserve draw may fund a payment but does not improve an operating-coverage numerator merely by being available. Show both the operating ratio and the actual funding sources.

## 4. Checked hypothetical order example

**New teaching case; not an observed business or an edit to the existing article example. All amounts are KES thousands.** Assume no tax or additional unrelated cash movements within the example. Opening cash is unrestricted. The chosen minimum cash balance of 100 is a planning assumption, not a regulatory rule.

The customer pays 200 upfront and 800 on day 60. A loan of 500 is drawn on day 0 with a fee of 10 paid immediately. The supplier receives 600 on day 0; staff receive 120 on day 30; completion costs of 100 fall due on day 60. Loan principal and simple interest at 18% annually, ACT/360, are due on day 60.

| Cash movement | Day 0 | Day 30 | Day 60 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Opening cash | 250 | 340 | 220 |
| Customer receipts | 200 | 0 | 800 |
| Loan drawn | 500 | 0 | 0 |
| Supplier, staff and completion payments | −600 | −120 | −100 |
| Upfront financing fee | −10 | 0 | 0 |
| Loan interest | 0 | 0 | −15 |
| Principal repaid | 0 | 0 | −500 |
| **Closing cash** | **340** | **220** | **405** |
| **Headroom above the assumed 100 minimum** | **240** | **120** | **305** |

Interest is `500 × 18% × 60/360 = 15`. Operating surplus is `1,000 − 600 − 120 − 100 = 180`. Financing costs are `10 + 15 = 25`. Incremental cash is `155`, reconciling opening 250 to closing 405. The 305 closing headroom is available in this deliberately closed example; a real distribution decision must include later obligations.

### Delay the receipt while keeping the contract unchanged

If the customer's final 800 arrives on day 90, day-60 closing cash becomes `220 − 100 − 15 − 500 = −395`. The shortfall to the assumed minimum of 100 is **495**. This is the funding gap before arranging any additional support. It does not assume an overdraft is available, and it is not an LGD estimate.

### Compare a facility agreed to mature on day 90

As a separate contractual alternative, assume the original 500 loan has a day-90 maturity, the same 10 upfront fee and all simple interest payable on day 90, with no further charges. Day-60 cash is 120. Day-90 interest is 22.5, and closing cash after the 800 receipt and debt repayment is 397.5. The assumed minimum is preserved at the displayed dates. The added financing cost is 7.5. This illustrates the value of matching maturity to collection; an extension of an existing facility would require agreement and its own charges and conditions.

The calendar aggregates transactions on each displayed date. A production schedule must capture intra-day payment order where it matters, and must extend beyond 13 weeks if debt or obligations run longer.

## 5. Future workbook specification

Extend the current `Debt and Growth` screen with a linked schedule containing:

1. Opening drawn balance; approved limit; drawable amount; availability conditions; commitment expiry and facility maturity.
2. Draws and repayments by date; interest base, rate, reset rules and day count; commitment and arrangement fees with a defined charging base.
3. Cash before financing, cash after contractual debt service, restricted reserves, permitted releases and closing unrestricted cash.
4. Covenant definitions and test dates; minimum-cash date; deficit against the chosen operating floor; proposed action and decision owner.
5. Base, receipt-delay, margin-reduction and supplier-terms scenarios. A blocked-draw scenario should change available funding, not merely the displayed limit.

Use separate columns for assumptions, contractual terms, calculated values and observed outcomes. Reconcile debt opening plus draws less principal repaid to debt closing, and cash opening plus sources less uses to cash closing. Loan proceeds are not revenue; principal repayment is not financing expense. Capitalise or expense fees in an accounting view only through an explicit bridge from the cash view.

Place the worked calendar after Part 4's “Work the debt decision from use to repayment,” with the calculation details in Appendix 3 and the workbook companion.

**Draft paragraph:** The useful borrowing amount depends on the dates as well as the margin. Follow one order from deposit to supplier payment, delivery and final collection, then place the lender's payments on that same calendar. Keep the business assumptions fixed while comparing facilities. Once the repayment structure is clear, delay the customer receipt and test what remains available. The result shows whether the owner needs a different maturity, a smaller draw, more committed liquidity or a change to the transaction itself.
