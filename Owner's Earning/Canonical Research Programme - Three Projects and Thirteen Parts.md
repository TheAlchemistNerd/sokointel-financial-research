# Owner Earnings: Canonical Three-Project, Thirteen-Part Research Programme

> **Status, 22 September 2026:** This is the active editorial and workbook blueprint, not thirteen finished articles or a built workbook. `Warren Buffett - Source Document.md` remains the faithful conversion of the original Word file; `Supplemental Source Record - MSME Operations and Failure Analysis.md` organises the newer screenshot and pasted-text material; and `Owner Earnings Research Questions and Eleven Part Series Outline.md` remains a traceable, superseded editorial record. ## Programme architecture

The shared question is what cash remains for owners after the business preserves its productive assets, working capital, people and capital base. The answer changes with business model and with the evidence available.

| Project | Parts | Intended decision | Output at the future publication stage |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **1. MSMEs and small businesses** | 1–5 | Measure, protect, reinvest and transfer the cash engine | Five articles and an interactive daily/strategic workbook |
| **2. Listed companies** | 6–10 | Reconstruct a dated owner cash claim and valuation range by sector | Five filing-led articles and company/sector workbook modules |
| **3. Failure retrospectives** | 11–13 | Test which warnings were knowable before distress | Three evidence-graded articles and a comparative warning model |

The working article budget is about 3,500 words per part, approximately 45,500 words in all, excluding citations, appendices and workbook instructions. Depth and source quality take priority over a fixed word count. Each part needs a decision question, a worked bridge or table, limitations, a future workbook mapping and a dated evidence ledger.

## Shared owner-earnings bridge: required calculation sequence

Every use of “owner earnings” must identify the period, currency and cash-flow claimant. The steps below are the editorial and model specification; no real-company result is calculated yet.

| Step | Component | Treatment and audit question |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | **Reported net income/profit** | Start with an audited or reconciled figure. Is it after interest and tax, hence an equity-oriented starting point? Tie it to the statement and period. |
| 2 | **D&A and other non-cash charges** | Add back depreciation, depletion and amortisation as applicable. Examine provisions, impairment, fair-value changes and deferred taxes individually; a non-cash charge may still represent a real future cost. Reconcile to the cash-flow statement. |
| 3 | **Maintenance capital expenditure** | Deduct a normalized range needed to preserve competitive position and unit volume. Compare asset register, replacement-cost and multi-year spending methods; document lives, inflation and uncertainty. |
| 4 | **Growth capital expenditure** | Reconcile maintenance plus growth to total cash capex. Excluding growth spending from a no-growth owner-earnings measure does not make expansion free: growth scenarios must fund it and test the incremental return. |
| 5 | **Required working-capital changes** | Deduct extra cash committed to receivables, inventory and other operating balances, net of sustainable payables/customer advances. Separate temporary releases from recurring earning power; distinguish contractual supplier credit from overdue arrears. |
| 6 | **MSME owner compensation** | Replace unusual, unpaid or above-market owner labour with documented market replacement cost. Reconcile reported profit, Seller's Discretionary Earnings (SDE) and normalized owner earnings without conflating them. |
| 7 | **Tax, debt and liquidity** | Test actual/normalized tax, interest, principal, covenants, restricted cash and minimum reserves before describing a prudent distribution. Do not subtract interest twice when beginning with after-interest net income. |
| 8 | **Corporate adjustments** | Examine stock-based compensation (SBC), dilution/buybacks, pensions, leases, deferred revenue obligations, acquisitions, minority interests and intangible reinvestment. If net income already expenses SBC, avoid a second deduction of the same charge. |
| 9 | **Valuation claim** | Label the stream as equity cash flow, firm cash flow or sector-specific distributable capital. Match discount rate to claim and bridge enterprise to equity value, debt, excess cash and share count when needed. |

Conceptually, equity-oriented owner earnings begin with after-tax profit, add justified non-cash charges, then deduct maintenance investment and additional operating capital required at the selected scale. This is an estimate of sustainable earning power, not automatically cash safe to withdraw. The finished work must show **period cash**, **normalized earning power** and **prudent distribution** separately. Buffett's exact 1986 wording must be retrieved from Berkshire Hathaway before quotation.

**Variants:** MSMEs require owner-labour and record-quality adjustments; retail and manufacturing require stock and replacement assets; software and NVIDIA-led technology require intangible reinvestment, SBC/dilution and customer-concentration analysis; asset-heavy infrastructure and data centres require equipment/lease/debt and maintenance-versus-capacity splits. Banks require normalized credit costs, loan growth and regulatory capital rather than industrial CCC/capex mechanics. Insurers require claims reserves, underwriting, investment returns and solvency capital. Property/REITs require FFO/AFFO, structural upkeep and fair-value separation. Utilities, agriculture, telecom and manufacturers require tariff/regulatory, biological-asset/commodity, spectrum/network and capacity/replacement adjustments respectively.

## Project 1 — MSMEs and small businesses

### Part 1 — The cash an owner can actually claim

**Decision:** Why can an owner report a profit yet lack safe drawing capacity? **Research:** Buffett's original definition; profit versus CFO, EBITDA, FCF and SDE; which cash claim belongs to whom; what non-cash add-backs really mean. **Article path:** a labelled hypothetical profit/cash puzzle, the nine-step bridge, three distinct outputs (period cash, normalized owner earnings, prudent distribution), then a glossary of category errors. **Future workbook:** `P1 Profit-to-Cash`, with editable highlighted inputs, source cells, sign checks and debt/reserve warnings. **Evidence gate:** the 1986 shareholder letter and applicable accounting standards, not AI paraphrases.

### Part 2 — Maintenance capital and working capital

**Decision:** How much must remain in the business simply to preserve current operations? **Research:** maintenance versus growth capex; replacement cost versus depreciation; recurring versus seasonal working capital; treatment of customer advances and supplier arrears. **Article path:** asset register and classification, multiple maintenance-capex estimation methods, AR/inventory/AP bridge and low/base/high range. **Future workbook:** `P2 Asset Register`, `P2 Maintenance Capex`, `P2 Working Capital`, with total-capex and balance-change reconciliation. **Evidence gate:** asset, invoice and contract records for a real MSME; do not adopt a generic maintenance/depreciation percentage.

### Part 3 — The daily MSME operating toolkit

**Decision:** Which cash shortfall, weak transaction, overdue invoice, stuck stock or supplier exposure demands action today? The dedicated article must include:

1. A **rolling 13-week cash forecast** of dated opening cash, receipts, payroll, suppliers, tax, rent, debt and closing cash, with the lowest-cash week and shortfall trigger.
2. **Gross/net burn and runway**, separating recurring, growth and one-off uses; zero or positive net burn must not produce a misleading runway number.
3. **Contribution margin and unit/revenue/cash break-even**, with explicit variable/fixed cost classification and debt/maintenance requirements where relevant.
4. **A/R aging and DSO**, using invoice due dates, credit sales, matched periods, disputed balances, concentration and contractual terms.
5. **Inventory aging, ABC classification, turnover, DIO, sales velocity, reorder points and holding costs**, with seasonality, spoilage, lead time and stock-outs visible.
6. **A/P aging and DPO**, supplier criticality and overdue status; `CCC = DSO + DIO - DPO` must be read with the underlying ledgers so distressed non-payment cannot masquerade as efficiency.
7. A dated action log testing the cash, margin and relationship effects of collection, discount, return, markdown, reorder or payment-term decisions.

**Future workbook:** `P3 13-Week Cash`, `P3 Burn and Runway`, `P3 Unit Economics`, `P3 AR Aging`, `P3 Inventory Aging and ABC`, `P3 AP Aging and CCC`, `P3 Action Log`. **Evidence gate:** operational ledgers and contracts; the supplied 3:1 LTV:CAC, 20–30% holding-cost, 60–90-day dead-stock and stop-supply thresholds remain unverified leads, not default rules.

### Part 4 — Owner pay, reserves, growth and debt

**Decision:** Draw cash, preserve it, borrow, hire or invest? **Research:** safe distributions after tax, debt and reserve; incremental return after capital and working-capital needs; loan tenor versus cash cycle or asset life; downside timing. **Article path:** normalize owner pay, set a reserve, model debt/covenant headroom and compare a growth project under delayed-receipt and supplier-withdrawal stress. **Future workbook:** `P4 Distribution and Reserve`, `P4 Growth Case`, `P4 Debt Timing`. **Evidence gate:** relevant loan contracts, tax rules and owner-pay records before real examples.

### Part 5 — Buying, selling and succession

**Decision:** What cash earning power transfers when the founder leaves? **Research:** EBITDA/SDE/owner-earnings reconciliation, documented add-backs, replacement management cost, customer/supplier dependence and acquisition debt capacity. **Article path:** buyer/seller/lender transaction bridge and a valuation range tied to normalized working capital, debt and surplus cash. **Future workbook:** `P5 Normalizations`, `P5 Transaction Bridge`, `P5 Debt Capacity`, `P5 Key-Person Sensitivity`. **Evidence gate:** document each adjustment; do not generalize a private-business multiple.

## Project 2 — listed companies across asset profiles

### Part 6 — Reconstructing owner earnings from public filings

**Decision:** What dated per-share cash-earning range can an investor defend? **Research:** income/cash-flow/balance-sheet links, non-cash items, maintenance versus growth capex, working-capital versus financing movements, claim type and share-count change. **Article path:** rebuild multi-year filing history, normalize cycle and one-offs, show low/base/high owner earnings, then introduce equity value, enterprise-to-equity and reverse-DCF checks without claiming precision. **Future workbook:** `P6 Filing Inputs`, `P6 Owner-Earnings Bridge`, `P6 Claim and Share Check`, `P6 Valuation Range`. **Evidence gate:** audited annual reports and filings, exact notes/pages, and a dated price and share count.

### Part 7 — US technology and asset-light economics, anchored by NVIDIA

**Decision:** What remains for owners after physical and intangible productive capacity is maintained? **Research:** NVIDIA's reported cash bridge, cyclicality, inventory, supply commitments and customer concentration; R&D and capitalized software; SBC, dilution/buybacks and deferred obligations. **Article path:** a dated NVIDIA filing reconstruction, then a selected software/platform comparison showing different asset and revenue economics. Stress demand, gross margin, reinvestment, working capital and share count. NVIDIA is an anchor for future research, not a current valuation conclusion. **Future workbook:** `P7 NVIDIA Filing Bridge`, `P7 Intangible Reinvestment`, `P7 SBC and Dilution`, `P7 Scenario Valuation`. **Evidence gate:** current SEC and issuer filings before any numerical claim.

### Part 8 — Asset-heavy issuers and data-centre infrastructure

**Decision:** Which cash spending preserves existing service and which creates new capacity? **Research:** physical replacement cycles, power/cooling, real estate, leases, financing and construction-in-progress; unit economics of an established asset-heavy issuer and a selected data-centre infrastructure issuer. The latter is the second planned topical sector; whether it is “trending” and which issuers make sound comparators must be checked when research begins. **Article path:** reconcile total capex into maintenance, compliance and growth ranges; model debt/lease claims, utilisation and customer concentration. Add an energy-transition or regulated-utility sidebar to contrast capital intensity under different revenue protections. **Future workbook:** `P8 Asset Register`, `P8 Maintenance-Growth Split`, `P8 Lease and Debt Claims`, `P8 Capacity Scenario`. **Evidence gate:** audited filings, capex and lease notes, contracts where available and a documented comparator selection.

### Part 9 — Banks and insurers: distributable capital

**Decision:** What can a financial institution distribute while remaining able to lend, absorb losses and pay claims? **Research:** bank loan growth, expected credit losses, funding and regulatory capital; insurer underwriting, claims reserves, investments, asset-liability matching and solvency. Explain why ordinary inventory/CCC and physical-capex formulas cannot simply be transferred to either institution. **Article path:** a bank normalized-earnings/excess-capital/residual-income bridge and an insurer underwriting/reserving/solvency bridge, each with stress cases and payout limits. **Future workbook:** `P9 Bank Capital`, `P9 Credit Stress`, `P9 Insurance Underwriting`, `P9 Reserves and Solvency`, `P9 Distributable Capital`. **Evidence gate:** audited reports, regulator returns and current capital rules.

### Part 10 — NSE cross-sector casebook and decision framework

**Decision:** How should Kenyan issuers be compared without one formula flattening their economics? **Research scope:** actual dated cases across **NSE banks, insurance, energy, agriculture, telecommunications, manufacturing and property**. The bank and insurer methods extend Part 9; non-financial cases apply the correct replacement, lease, commodity, biological-asset, spectrum, network, capacity, property and fair-value adjustments. **Article path:** a source-linked bridge and low/base/high range per selected issuer, plus a cross-sector matrix of cash claim, adjustment sensitivity, governance, currency, state influence, liquidity and evidence quality. Compare the estimate with dividends and price at a defined date; no forced ranking by a single ratio. **Future workbook:** `P10 NSE Sources`, sector sheets, `P10 Cross-Sector Dashboard`, `P10 Decision Range`. **Evidence gate:** NSE/CMA, issuer and sector-regulator primary records; select actual issuers only after evidence sufficiency is assessed.

## Project 3 — failure retrospectives and warning systems

### Part 11 — The retrospective method

**Decision:** Was a warning visible at the time, or only after the outcome was known? **Research:** period owner earnings and burn; DSO, DIO, DPO and supplier arrears; unrestricted funding runway and debt claims; governance decisions; customer and supplier behaviour; alternative explanations. **Article path:** define an evidence hierarchy and contemporaneous-information cutoffs before any case. Build a common cash bridge, CCC table, funding register (separating announced commitments from disbursements; equity, debt and grants; legal obligor, currency, FX date, maturity and recoveries), decision timeline and counterparty map. Mark fact, management claim, secondary report, analyst estimate, hypothesis and missing data separately. “Insufficient evidence” is a legitimate conclusion. **Future workbook:** `P11 Evidence Ledger`, `P11 Timeline`, `P11 Cash and CCC Bridge`, `P11 Funding Runway`, `P11 Alternative Explanations`. **Evidence gate:** court, administrator, audited, regulator, contract and company material ahead of narrative reports; source cards are leads only.

### Part 12 — Nakumatt and Tuskys retail retrospectives

**Decision:** Did stock, supplier financing, expansion and governance make the retail cash cycle fragile before visible collapse? **Research:** dated store, inventory, payables, borrowing, capex and supplier-delivery records; contractual DPO versus arrears; management and governance events. **Article path:** separate company chronologies and bounded cash bridges, then a like-for-like comparison. Establish whether stop-supply, empty shelves, closures and governance decisions preceded or followed liquidity stress. Rate what a manager, supplier, lender and outside analyst could actually know at each date. **Future workbook:** `P12 Nakumatt Evidence`, `P12 Tuskys Evidence`, `P12 Retail CCC`, `P12 Timeline Comparison`. **Evidence gate:** supplemental claims about store counts, revenue, stock shortfalls and causation must be verified in primary materials; do not invent a precise historical owner-earnings total where records are incomplete.

### Part 13 — Twiga, a verified startup comparator and cross-case warnings

**Decision:** Did external capital cover a weak operating cash engine, and which warning signals could have prompted action before runway ran out? **Research:** Twiga's verified legal/operating status by date; inventory ownership, spoilage, customer credit, supplier terms, infrastructure, unit contribution, funding rounds and governance. Choose **Copia or Zumi** as the second startup case only after a documented primary-source quality screen; record why the other was not selected. **Article path:** dated case timelines, confidence-tagged cash/runway reconstructions and a comparative warning matrix across the startup cases, Nakumatt and Tuskys. Include owner earnings, contribution, DSO, DIO, DPO, overdue payables, debt service, runway, governance overrides, supplier response and customer collection; rate each signal's contemporaneous visibility, false-positive risk and practical response. September 2026 reporting identifies GT Flow Limited (formerly Twiga Foods One Limited) and Templar Field Limited (formerly Twiga Foods Limited) as under administration. Obtain and inspect the Kenya Gazette notice and administrator releases before publication; administration is not liquidation, and the ultimate outcome for creditors or wider operations remains unresolved. The related [startup-funding companion project](What%20Startup%20Funding%20Figures%20Actually%20Measure/README.md) examines the market-level figures and the difference between funding raised and losses. **Future workbook:** `P13 Twiga Evidence`, `P13 Candidate Selection`, `P13 Startup Cash and Runway`, `P13 Supplier-Customer Map`, `P13 Cross-Case Warnings`. **Evidence gate:** direct confirmation of legal status, funding totals, exchange conversions and causal claims; if financial records are insufficient, publish bounded or qualitative findings.

## Future companion-workbook specification

These modules are a blueprint, **not built workbooks**. They must allow readers to replace clearly highlighted example inputs and observe live, formula-driven changes. Every released model needs common `Guide`, `Sources`, `Assumptions`, `Checks`, `Change Log` and `Verification Cases` sheets in addition to the part-specific modules above.

| Cell class | Behaviour | Audit requirement |
|---|---|---|
| **User input** | Highlighted, unlocked and validated | Label, unit, period, scenario, input owner and practical help text |
| **Sourced input** | Visually distinct from assumptions; controlled editing | Company, exact document, note/page, URL or local path, period, retrieval date, currency, units and transformation |
| **Formula** | Protected and calculated live | Dependency, sign convention, missing-input handling and independent benchmark |
| **Review output** | Distinct result or warning style | Interpretation, confidence, trigger rule and links to input/source |

Controls include reset to a labelled hypothetical example; low/base/high scenarios; period, currency and display-unit selectors that do not silently change the economics; structured ranges that expand when rows are added; validation for dates, rates, probabilities, useful lives and non-negative quantities; protected formulas; no hidden plugs; no external links required for core calculations; and print-ready owner, lender and investor summaries. Monthly, quarterly and annual ratios must use matched periods. For retrospective cases, `not evidenced` is a valid output; a formula must not infer missing company data from a headline. Sensitivity tables must recompute from inputs rather than contain pasted outcomes.

Before future release, independently recompute major outputs, test that changing each editable input alters the intended result, test invalid/missing inputs and extreme scenarios, confirm balance/cash-flow/capex/claim/share-count checks, recalculate all formulas without broken links, and inspect normal-zoom, print and PDF layouts. The article's compact example must use the same values and terminology as its workbook sheet. The `Sources` sheet is a full source ledger, not a list of publisher names from search cards.

## Evidence and publication controls

1. Retrieve primary evidence first: original shareholder letters, audited statements, official filings, court/administrator records, regulators, contracts and dated company or investor releases. Record exact title, issuer, date, reporting period, page/note, URL or local path, retrieval date, currency, units and transformations.
2. Use secondary reporting to discover primary material, triangulate chronology and describe reaction. Search cards, snippets, social posts and AI responses are leads only, never final authority.
3. Tag every real-company statement as **verified fact**, **management assertion**, **secondary report**, **analyst estimate**, **hypothesis** or **unresolved**. Preserve method, range, alternatives and confidence for estimates.
4. Use dated market prices, matched cash-flow claims and value ranges. Keep hypothetical numbers visibly hypothetical. Do not universalise private multiples, current-ratio bands, LTV:CAC, stock-holding percentages or collection cutoffs.
5. In retrospect, maintain a contemporaneous-information column. Do not infer insolvency, legal status or causal collapse from one AI narrative or from an adverse outcome alone.
6. Eventual articles should use IEEE-style numbered references; the research ledger and workbook hold the full audit trail.
