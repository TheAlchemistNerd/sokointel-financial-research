**1. What is a "Country of Tax Residence"?**

Your **country of tax residence** is the country where you are legally obligated to pay income tax on your global earnings. It is determined by where you physically live or work, not necessarily your nationality.

- **The Rule:** Since you reside in **Kenya**, your tax residence is Kenya.

- **The Impact:** Kenya does not have a double taxation treaty with the United States. If you buy US-domiciled ETFs (like **SPYM** or **QQQ**), the US government will automatically strip **30% withholding tax** from your dividends before the cash reaches your account.

**2. Is it Only Ireland for Non-US Domiciled ETFs?**

No, but **Ireland is the absolute best option** for US equity investing. European UCITS ETFs can be domiciled in Ireland, Luxembourg, or Germany. However, investors choose Ireland for one specific *in situ* tax optimization reason: **The US-Ireland Tax Treaty**.

- **Ireland:** Has a special treaty with the US. When an Irish fund holds Apple, Microsoft, or NVIDIA, the US only charges the fund **15% tax** on dividends.

- **Luxembourg:** Does not have this specific clause for ETFs. A Luxembourg-domiciled S&P 500 ETF is hit with the full **30% US withholding tax** inside the fund.

- **The Verdict:** If you want to track the S&P 500 or Nasdaq from Kenya, **always choose an Irish-domiciled (IE) ETF** to automatically save 15% on dividend drag.

**Step 1: Fund your Wise Account in KES**

1.  Open your Wise app and select your **KES balance** (or create one).

3.  Enter the amount of KES you want to move.

4.  Select **Debit Card** or **Credit Card** as your payment method. Use your standard Kenyan bank debit card (Visa or Mastercard).

5.  Wise processes this internally as a local card transaction. Your Kenyan bank will simply see a card purchase, avoiding heavy outgoing wire transfer fees.

**Step 2: Convert KES to USD Inside Wise**

1.  Once your KES lands in Wise, tap **Convert**.

2.  Select KES to USD.

3.  Wise uses the mid-market exchange rate (the real one you see on Google) and charges a transparent, fractional fee (typically around 0.4% to 0.6%). This is significantly cheaper than the 3% to 5% spread hidden inside Kenyan bank exchange rates.

5.  Click confirm. The money moves via a local US ACH transfer behind the scenes. It bypasses the global SWIFT network entirely, costing you pennies and landing in your trading account almost instantly.
