# Research questions for the two-part LinkedIn series

Prepared 7 September 2026. Starting point: [the original African markets document](<../why are African investors bullish on Kenya e.md>).

## Five research questions

1. **What does verified investment activity reveal about Kenya's appeal to African infrastructure investors and banking groups?** Distinguish operating assets, completed acquisitions, regulatory approvals, financing proposals, and actual new capital entering businesses.
2. **Under what conditions can regional energy and transport infrastructure improve trade, business productivity, and currency resilience?** Examine reliability, operating costs, market access, financing obligations, import content, and the distribution of benefits.
3. **How do bank ownership, funding, credit quality, and Kenya's loan-pricing reforms affect SME access to finance?** Evaluate competition, underwriting, capital, foreign-currency exposure, and the difference between a benchmark rate and the total cost of borrowing.
4. **When does fintech credit help businesses grow, and when do its costs and repayment terms increase financial stress?** Separate payment services, household liquidity, working capital, and long-term investment; test repayment timing and total cost.
5. **What should investors, business owners, and policymakers measure to determine whether financial expansion is producing broader economic benefits?** Focus on productive lending, business survival, borrower welfare, competition, and evidence of additional economic activity.

## Article structure

- [Part 1; Following African Capital into Kenya](<Part 1 - African Capital and Kenya.md>): questions 1 and 2, with the bank ownership and funding foundations of question 3.
- [Part 2; Making Business Credit Work in Kenya](<Part 2 - Business Credit and Fintech.md>): questions 3 and 4, followed by a practical measurement framework answering question 5.

Each article has an independent IEEE-style numbered reference list. In-text numbers link directly to online sources. Peer-reviewed journal studies are identified in the references. Worked financial examples are original illustrations, not market quotations or observed business results.

## Scope and evidence decisions

- **KPC is excluded from both articles**, following the requested scope change. Its IPO subscription quality and reported government pressure on public entities would require a separate governance investigation. Business Daily's August 31, 2026 report is a reported allegation, not an official adjudication: [report](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/markets/capital-markets/how-state-firms-were-forced-to-buy-kenya-pipeline-shares-5576870).
- **Dangote refinery:** retain as a reported proposal. Reuters' July 7, 2026 interview with a company executive supports a proposed 700,000-barrel-per-day facility and financing intentions; it does not establish financial close, commissioning, an exact final cost, or the original document's claimed public seed contribution.
- **Nedbank and NCBA:** distinguish the January offer from August regulatory approval. NCBA's September 1 notice says conditions remain. Do not describe the acquisition as fully settled based on these notices.
- **Access and NBK:** the May 2025 corporate announcement describes completion, but the June 2025 financial statements say accounting control had not yet transferred. Attribute both to their dates rather than merging the two statements into an unqualified chronology.
- **FDI:** the verified UNCTAD World Investment Report 2025 Kenya fact sheet reports 2024 inward flows of USD 1.503 billion. It cannot support an undated claim of USD 3.2 billion. Later reports may revise earlier estimates; this series does not present the older publication as the latest 2026 release.
- **Currency:** reserves and a stable exchange rate are dated observations. They do not establish that the shilling will remain stable through a future election.
- **Loan pricing:** KESONIA is a reference benchmark. The premium and charges require separate assessment. Exemption of foreign-currency loans from this benchmark does not establish freedom from regulation or automatically higher profitability.
- **Capital requirements:** discuss the direction of reform and the documented 2024 framework historically. Do not present the original 2029 timetable as an unquestioned current deadline, given subsequent proposals to change implementation.
- **Digital-credit regulation:** distinguish the 2022 regulations from later draft regulations and impact assessments. A draft is not proof that every proposed provision is in force.
- **Peer-reviewed evidence:** use journal studies to support mechanisms and findings in their studied populations. Do not project household mobile-money findings directly into guaranteed SME profitability or current project forecasts.
