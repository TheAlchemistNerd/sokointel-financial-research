# Making Business Credit Work in Kenya: additional pricing and treasury notes

**23 September 2026 · Two-paste review · Editorial proposals**

The current article is `Part 2 - Business Credit and Fintech.md` in this folder. It already follows a financed order through product choice, benchmark pricing, evidence, total cost, economic entity resolution and repayment. The useful next material should deepen that business story without turning it into a Treasury textbook.

## Three additions that fit the article

### 1. After “Put two offers on the same piece of paper”

Add a small table showing net cash received, all fees, payment dates, unused-line charges if relevant, and the receipt expected to repay each obligation. Explain both total financing cost and the lowest-cash date. A cheaper annual rate can coexist with an unsuitable repayment calendar.

**Proposed paragraph:**

The loan's price and the order's cash timing belong on the same page. Write down what reaches the business after upfront charges, then every payment required and the customer receipt expected to fund it. If the final invoice is paid a month late, does the business have a reserve, a usable commitment or another documented source of cash? This turns the comparison from a headline-rate exercise into a decision about the order the business can safely deliver.

### 2. After “Build the facility around the problem”

Use the revolving case to explain that unused capacity may still have a price and conditions. A limit is not the same as cash in the account. Include expiry, review, drawing conditions and availability during stress. Keep the lender's capital model in an optional technical companion, while the article concentrates on the owner's decision.

### 3. After “Follow the borrower beyond disbursement”

Link actual collection evidence to future financing terms. Explain how a reliable receipt history can improve the discussion about term, limit and structure without promising an automatic lower rate. A guarantee can help allocate loss but does not supply the customer's payment on demand.

**Proposed paragraph:**

A guarantee can change who bears a loss, while the business still needs to complete the order and meet the agreed payments. Its value depends on which facility is covered, the conditions for a valid claim and when settlement occurs. The useful conversation is therefore about both protection and continuity: what support remains available while the business is waiting for its customer to pay?
